# Short Original Content and Third-Party Source Rights Protocol

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 28 October 2026  
**Status:** Binding production rule for English learning content; applies across programme tracks and media types.  
**Related source of truth:** PROJECT_MASTER_BRIEF.md, ENGLISH_PUBLIC_SOURCE_REGISTER.md, ENGLISH_STAGE_1_ROADMAP.md, and the Unit Production Register.

## 1. Purpose and default decision

Use a proportionate, evidence-based process that protects learners and the platform without making every short, internally created passage wait for a formal legal opinion.

**Original content is deliberately limited to short, purpose-built prose passages/excerpts and the functional material needed to teach or assess them** (questions, instructions, explanations, brief model reasoning and feedback). Do not use original long-form reading passages as a shortcut around sourcing suitable literature or other texts.

Longer passages/excerpts and stimulus artefacts used in tasks—such as photographs, illustrations, posters, adverts, notices, infographics, book/page images and other documentary or visual material—must normally come from a specific work verified as public domain or available under an open licence that permits the actual intended use. Record provenance and rights **before selecting or embedding the exact asset**. A short platform-authored caption, prompt, or minimal overlay may accompany a rights-cleared source asset; it must not turn into an original long-form stimulus or obscure the source's conditions.

The procedure is an internal content-control standard, not legal advice or a guarantee that a court would reach a particular result.

## 2. Short original passages and accompanying tasks

For a short passage written/commissioned for this platform:

1. Record the content ID/version, that it was created for the platform, who selected/edited/approved it, and the source basis (normally “platform-original; no third-party passage or model answer intentionally reproduced”).
2. Run a proportionate originality screen at initial creation/revision: check distinctive phrases or any wording that raises a concrete concern against reasonably accessible public results. Repeating broad searches on unchanged text is unnecessary.
3. Have the responsible human reviewer check the exact learner-facing version for academic accuracy, age/level, cultural/context suitability and fit for the task.
4. If the screen finds no concerning match, no third-party source is known, and the human review approves the text, record **Internal originality/provenance screen: PASSED** and continue to the other release gates. Do not demand a separate formal legal review for each ordinary short original passage when no specific risk indicator is present.
5. If a distinctive or substantial match, unrecorded borrowed source, uncertain adaptation, disputed ownership, or other concrete rights concern appears, pause that item and investigate, rewrite it, or record an appropriate licence/permission/legal basis before use.

An exact-phrase search that finds no match is supporting evidence only: it is not comprehensive plagiarism detection, proof of authorship, or a legal opinion. For AI-assisted drafting, record human selection/editing and approval; do not promise that AI output is legally exclusive or that its copyright status is settled merely because it was generated internally.

## 3. Longer texts, public-domain works and open-licensed assets

Before selecting the exact text/image/poster/document:

- Identify the exact work and version/edition, its creator, publication/source page, and relevant dates.
- Verify the public-domain basis for the relevant jurisdiction **or** capture the exact open licence and version, including whether commercial online display, reproduction, modification/cropping and redistribution are permitted for the proposed use.
- Check separate rights where applicable: translation, adaptation, modern edition/annotations, illustrations, photographs within a work, embedded logos, recognisable people and private/property/model releases.
- Record the source URL, access/check date, licence or public-domain evidence, attribution text required, edits made, and the reviewer who checked it.
- Preserve the original source/rights evidence with the unit record. Attribution is required when the licence requires it, but attribution alone does not create permission.
- Treat “copyright free” as a description to verify, not proof. A search result, repost, public availability, school use, or “for educational purposes” label is not itself a rights basis.

For images and other visual files, the exact file and licence must be checked **before selection or embedding**, not retrospectively. Prefer CC0/public-domain assets or other licences that clearly permit all intended uses. If the terms or jurisdiction are uncertain, choose a clearer asset rather than holding up the whole unit.

## 4. Existing copyrighted material

Do not reproduce a longer copyrighted passage or a third-party stimulus by default. Use it only after recording either specific permission/licence covering the intended use or a clearly documented legal basis approved for that use. Educational purpose alone should not be treated as automatic permission. Obtain legal advice only where a material, specific uncertainty cannot be resolved by choosing an original short passage or a verifiably reusable source.

## 5. Record separate outcomes, not one vague “clearance” label

Use the most precise status:

- **Internal originality/provenance screen — Passed:** short platform-original text was checked proportionately; no unresolved indicator was identified. This is not a formal legal opinion or guarantee.
- **Source licence/public-domain basis — Verified:** the exact third-party work/file and the rights for the intended use were documented.
- **Permission/legal basis — Recorded:** the permission or other specific legal basis for the particular use is documented.
- **Formal legal opinion — Obtained:** use only if a qualified legal adviser actually supplied one.
- **Blocked/escalated:** a material source, licence, ownership, scope or adaptation concern remains unresolved.

A routine internal originality screen and a source-licence check are not interchangeable. A public-domain/open-licence source still needs exact-source verification; a short original text does not need an invented third-party licence record.

## 6. Required unit evidence

The unit register/QA record should identify:
- Exact learner content version and review date.
- Whether each stimulus is short platform-original text or a sourced work/asset.
- For original short text: provenance statement, the scope/date/result of any phrase screen, human approval, and any concern investigated.
- For third-party assets: exact source/work, public-domain or open-licence evidence, allowed-use conditions, required attribution, modifications, and pre-selection check date.
- Separate curriculum-mapping, human review, accessibility and release statuses.

Do not repeat the same search merely to increase the number of searches when the content has not changed and there is no new concern. Re-run the relevant check if the text/asset changes materially, a source concern arises, or the intended use/licence changes.
