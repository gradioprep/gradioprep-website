# Literary Excerpt and Content Rights Protocol

**Version:** 2.1  
**Last updated:** 30 October 2026  
**Status:** Binding production rule for all English programme tracks and all reading passages/excerpts authored or materially revised from this update forward.  
**Supersedes:** Version 2.0's blanket requirement that every short passage must be public domain. The founder-approved 250-word threshold below is now controlling. Existing content is not to be rewritten solely because this threshold changed.  
**Related standards:** `docs/TEACHER_PACK_AND_FEEDBACK_STANDARD.md`, `docs/LITERARY_EXCERPT_SOURCE_REGISTER.md`, `docs/ENGLISH_PUBLIC_SOURCE_REGISTER.md`, `docs/ENGLISH_STAGE_1_ROADMAP.md`, and `docs/curriculum/UNIT_PRODUCTION_REGISTER.md`.


## 1. Binding passage-length rule

For every learner-facing reading passage/stimulus:

- **250 words or fewer (including exactly 250):** a platform-original passage written for the project is allowed. Existing platform-original passages in this length band may remain unchanged in the content/question bank. New platform-original passages in this length band may also be authored from this point forward.
- **More than 250 words (251+):** use an excerpt from a recognised public-domain work by a well-known writer. Verify the specific work/edition/transcription and public-domain status for the intended distribution territories, and register the exact excerpt before use.
- This short-passage allowance applies to text authored by the project. It does **not** permit copying a third-party copyrighted passage or a modern translation merely because the copied text is 250 words or fewer. Any externally sourced material still needs a verified public-domain, permission or other expressly approved rights basis.
- Visual/documentary assets remain governed by their separate, stricter source and licence controls. This threshold does not relax the photograph rule.

**Word-count method for the 250-word threshold:** count the body of the reading stimulus only. Exclude its title/byline, question prompts, instructions, captions, source notes and surrounding teaching copy. Count lexical words containing letters or digits; punctuation by itself is not a word. A contraction or hyphenated compound counts as one word (for example, “don't” and “well-known”). Apply the same convention consistently when auditing existing passages.

The threshold applies to new writing and to material when it is next authored or materially revised. Do not alter an already-created passage solely because the standard has changed. In particular, the currently founder-approved MYP Year 1 Unit 1 v3 content remains unchanged by this update.

The project may continue to write original supporting teaching and assessment material at any length: instructions, question prompts, scaffolds, explanations, hints, answer rationales, model responses, feedback, rubrics and navigation copy. Such material must not reproduce protected modern wording or pass itself off as a quotation from an author.


## 2. Short-original and long-public-domain source checks

### A. Platform-original passage of 250 words or fewer

Before use, record:
1. A stable Excerpt ID, unit/content version and linked question/task IDs.
2. The exact word count under the method in section 1.
3. Author/owner (project/team), creation date and version history.
4. A proportionate distinctive-phrase originality/provenance screen, recording the searched phrases, date, scope and result.
5. A brief suitability check for vocabulary, syntax, meaning in context, age/level and intended skill.
6. Any concern or material later edit that requires another screen/review.

No public-domain Source ID is needed for text authored by the project. Do not invent a third-party source record for original text.

### B. Passage longer than 250 words

Before adopting the exact excerpt:
1. Select a recognised writer and a specific public-domain work that is stylistically, syntactically, semantically and thematically useful for the intended learner, skill and question type.
2. Confirm exact work/publication/creator details from a reliable source. Check public-domain status in the intended use territories, including India and any other territory in which the platform is intended to operate. Do not assume age, fame, online availability or a search-result label proves public-domain status.
3. Use the original-language text where practical. A modern translation, edited edition, annotation, illustration, introduction, transcription or adaptation can have separate rights even when the underlying work is public domain. Check the specific version being used.
4. Prefer a reliable public-domain text repository or digitised primary edition that identifies the work and edition. Record the exact page/URL and relevant chapter, section, stanza, poem or page locator.
5. Check vocabulary and syntax load, length, meaning in context, age suitability, cultural/context sensitivity, and whether the passage genuinely supports the intended skill.
6. Record the check date, reviewer, stable Excerpt ID, separate Source ID and full source rationale in the Literary Excerpt Source Register **before** placing the excerpt into learner or teacher content.

Do not use an unverified modern translation or silently modernise wording. Do not present a paraphrase as an exact quotation. Preserve the source's wording, punctuation and relevant context; use ellipses or bracketed changes transparently and sparingly.


## 3. Stable passage, source and replacement controls

Every passage location in the product has a stable **Excerpt ID**. Every platform-original short passage (250 words or fewer) must have an authorship/provenance record linked to that Excerpt ID. Every longer public-domain excerpt must have both the stable Excerpt ID and a separate **Source ID** identifying the exact public-domain work, edition/transcription and excerpt. Keep the Excerpt ID when a passage is replaced; create a new Source ID when the long-form source work, edition or excerpt changes.

The register must make it possible to replace a passage without changing the website's layout, routes, question flow, answer submission contract, teacher/learner separation or other functionality. Keep reading text in versioned content data and use stable question IDs and excerpt IDs. Update task wording, expected answers, model responses, feedback and provenance references when a passage changes; do not assume an old answer key still applies.

If a source is challenged or later found unsuitable, mark it blocked/replacement-required, choose a compliant replacement, and update only the content/source mapping and any passage-dependent assessment data. Preserve the previous version and source record for audit history.

## 4. Visual and documentary assets remain separately controlled

This text-excerpt rule does not relax the existing visual-asset rules. The project-specific photograph requirement remains binding: do not select, download, embed, commit or preview a photograph unless the exact file page explicitly confirms CC0 dedication or public-domain status, and screen visible third-party rights concerns before selection. For non-photographic visual assets, verify the exact source and licence permits all intended commercial/online uses and edits. Check image rights separately even when the accompanying literary work is public domain.


## 5. Rights outcomes and scope

Use precise, separate statuses:

- **Original short passage — provenance recorded:** project-authored stimulus of 250 words or fewer, with Excerpt ID, word count, author/date/version, proportionate originality/provenance screen and suitability note.
- **Public-domain long passage — verified:** exact work and edition/version over 250 words checked for the intended territory and fully recorded with Source ID evidence.
- **Source/edition mismatch — blocked:** the underlying work may be public domain, but the supplied translation, edition, transcription or asset is not verified.
- **Replacement required:** a passage over 250 words is not a verified public-domain excerpt, or any passage is otherwise unsuitable or has an unresolved rights concern.
- **Permission/licence basis — recorded:** a separate permission/licence is used for a third-party asset and its scope is documented.
- **Escalated:** a material rights, ownership, territory or adaptation concern cannot be resolved by selecting a clearer source.

This internal process is a practical content-control check, not legal advice or a guarantee of a particular legal outcome. Do not conduct extensive legal research as a routine substitute for selecting a clearly public-domain long passage. Escalate only a concrete unresolved issue.


## 6. Mandatory source-register fields

For every passage, record the stable Excerpt ID, content version, word count, source type (platform-original-short or public-domain-long), linked unit/question/task IDs and review status.

For a platform-original passage of 250 words or fewer, also record author/owner, creation date, version, distinctive-phrase originality/provenance screen, suitability note and any replacement history. No public-domain Source ID is required.

For a passage over 250 words, also record the separate Source ID, work title, author, relevant author/work dates and original language; exact edition/transcription/editor/translator; repository/publisher and URL; public-domain basis and territory/territories checked; exact chapter/section/page and opening/closing words or text fingerprint; exact stored-text pointer; omissions/formatting changes; suitability note; reviewer/check date; approval state and replacement history.

The excerpt provenance register is maintained at docs/LITERARY_EXCERPT_SOURCE_REGISTER.md. The GitHub repository is public, so that file must contain public-safe provenance metadata only; do not store confidential permissions, private legal advice, credentials or teacher-only answer keys there. Keep restricted evidence in an approved private store. Complete the relevant provenance/source record before a passage is inserted into a learner or teacher pack.

## 8. Quality, review and release remain separate

A verified public-domain source does not automatically make a passage academically suitable, correctly mapped, age-appropriate, accessible or released. After replacement, create a new content version and re-run the relevant academic, source-fit, question/answer consistency, accessibility, cultural/context and release checks. Preserve official curriculum mapping as unverified wherever the exact current official source is not available.
