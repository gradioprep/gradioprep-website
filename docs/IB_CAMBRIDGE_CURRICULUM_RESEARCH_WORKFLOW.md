# IB and Cambridge Curriculum Research Workflow

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Binding internal workflow for every level and course in the approved English scope  
**Owner:** Curriculum director / academic lead  
**Last reviewed:** 26 October 2026  
**Applies to:** IB MYP Language and Literature Years 1–5; IB DP Language A: Language and Literature platform delivery Years 1–2; Cambridge Lower Secondary English (0861) Stages 7–9; Cambridge IGCSE First Language English (0500), with platform delivery bands treated as internal sequencing rather than separate qualifications.

## 1. Standing access decision — do not ask the founder again

The founder has confirmed that they do **not** have access to an authorised school account or gated IB Programme Resource Centre (PRC) resources. This is a known constraint, not an outstanding request.

- Do not repeatedly ask the founder to obtain, upload, or arrange access to school-account-only curriculum frameworks, teacher guides, specimen exams, mark schemes or PRC resources.
- Do not block research or curriculum-guide development while waiting for gated documents.
- The curriculum director is responsible for finding the best defensible way forward from publicly accessible official sources, reputable specialist teaching/EdTech platforms, publishers' public samples and evidence-informed education research.
- If a source is genuinely inaccessible, record the attempted source, access limitation and what remains unverified. Continue with the best available evidence and label the resulting design accurately.
- Only ask the founder for a decision that genuinely requires their approval (for example, a scope change or approval to proceed to a new unit), not for access they have already said they do not possess.

## 2. Required research sequence for every course and level

Complete and record these steps independently for each course, stage/year and applicable syllabus edition. Do not assume evidence for one level or curriculum transfers to another.

1. **Define the exact route.** Record programme, subject/course, year/stage, official syllabus or framework code, applicable exam series/edition, intended learner profile and whether the level label is official or only an internal delivery band.
2. **Establish the current official baseline.** Search official public programme/course pages, syllabus PDFs, framework pages, assessment pages, specimen papers, mark schemes, examiner instructions/reports, syllabus-change notices and official FAQs. Record URL, displayed publication/update date, edition/session, date checked and the exact claims each source supports.
3. **Check currency explicitly.** Prefer the source that applies to the learner's target session. Compare older guides and papers with current public material; never call an older resource current merely because it is official. Where the latest detailed IB guide is gated or its status cannot be verified, distinguish current public framework statements from historical descriptors and leave exact official mapping unverified.
4. **Conduct level-specific specialist-platform research.** Compare relevant EdTech/teaching platforms, reputable publishers and specialist teacher resources for this precise curriculum and level. Examine their publicly visible course structures, skill progression, unit sequence, question/task types, worked examples, feedback model, differentiation, formative/summative assessment, teacher/learner workflow and evidence of syllabus alignment. Use representative public pages, samples, reviews or demonstrations where accessible. Record access/paywall limits and do not infer unseen content.
5. **Triangulate, do not copy.** Compare the specialist approaches against official public requirements and credible research on explicit instruction, modelling, scaffolded practice, retrieval, feedback, metacognition, accessibility and learner development. Explain why each adopted design choice is appropriate for the level. Platform popularity, marketing language or a publisher's endorsement alone is not proof of syllabus alignment.
6. **Design the implementation structure.** Produce a level-specific curriculum guide and implementation plan that specify intended learning outcomes in plain language; a coherent progression and unit sequence; proposed teaching/learning cycle; text/resource types; modelled, guided and independent practice; assessment and feedback; differentiation/accessibility; estimated learning time; mastery/transfer checks; and traceability to source evidence. Shared platform templates are allowed, but content, sequencing, assessment and mapping must remain curriculum- and level-specific.
7. **Map only what the evidence supports.** Link official codes, criteria, assessment objectives and syllabus claims only to directly verified sources. If a code/descriptor cannot be verified publicly, use a clearly labelled internal skill statement or provisional crosswalk and mark the official mapping as unverified. Never invent official identifiers or imply the platform's proposed sequence is prescribed by IB/Cambridge.
8. **Record confidence and limitations.** For each material claim/design decision, record source, course/level/edition, date checked, supporting evidence, cross-check, confidence and status (verified official; supported public framework; independent design recommendation; legacy reference; or unresolved). Identify the exact evidence needed to raise confidence, but do not make that evidence a blocker when a defensible public-source design can proceed.
9. **Run separate quality gates.** Academic/content review, official mapping/currency review, rights/originality, accessibility in the actual learner interface, age/level/cultural suitability, and release approval are distinct gates. Research completion does not itself clear any of them. Public availability does not grant permission to reproduce proprietary text, questions, answers, illustrations or platform materials.
10. **Checkpoint before the next unit.** Present the completed guide, implementation structure, source comparison, limitations, confidence rating and proposed next action. Obtain the founder's explicit approval before beginning each Unit 1 implementation/adaptation or other scope-gated unit work. Respect the approved curriculum-rotation order. Do not merge, publish or release without the applicable approvals.

## 3. Curriculum-specific rules

### IB (MYP and DP)
- Start with current public IB programme/subject pages, publicly accessible assessment information, subject briefs, public examiner instructions and any official public updates. Check relevant independent IB-specialist platforms and publishers for teaching structure and assessment interpretation.
- For MYP, do not treat the public six-skill overview as a complete Year-specific objective/strand map. If detailed current guide/PRC resources are inaccessible, build a public-framework-aligned, evidence-informed internal plan and state the mapping limitation prominently.
- For DP Language A: Language and Literature, preserve its distinction from Language A: Literature, Language B and Literature and Performance. Check the exact course, session, paper and criterion before applying examiner guidance or sample-response analysis.
- Do not assume IB prescribes the platform's proposed unit order. Treat platform Years 1–2 in DP as delivery chapters, not separate official syllabuses.
- Track official change/beta/transition notices and distinguish announced future changes from current requirements.

### Cambridge
- Start with the exact official subject code and the syllabus edition for the intended examination series. Use current public syllabus, specimen papers, mark schemes, examiner reports and change notices wherever available.
- For Cambridge Lower Secondary, confirm the specific subject and stage against public curriculum information and any accessible official stage objectives; do not assume a school-account resource is available.
- For Cambridge IGCSE First Language English, use syllabus 0500 only within the approved scope, and validate task formats and mark allocations against the target-series syllabus and current specimens. Older past papers are supporting practice only when their format/objectives have been checked against the current syllabus.
- Cambridge delivery bands or grade-equivalent labels are internal navigation/sequence aids, not separate qualifications or syllabus routes.

## 4. Source and resource evaluation rubric

Score each candidate resource from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) on: (a) relevance to exact curriculum and level; (b) currency and traceability; (c) quality of pedagogy and progression; (d) usefulness of examples, practice and feedback; (e) differentiation/accessibility; (f) transparency of authorship/evidence and access limits; and (g) practical suitability for the platform's budget and learner context. Provide short evidence-based rationales, not scores alone. Do not combine official authority and teaching usefulness into one score: an official syllabus is authoritative for requirements, while a specialist platform may be useful for implementation design but cannot establish official requirements.

## 5. Minimum deliverables per level

- Source and currency audit with working URLs and date checked.
- Comparison of relevant specialist platforms/publishers/resources, including what is visible publicly and what remains inaccessible.
- Curriculum guide and proposed unit/skill progression.
- Implementation structure with learning cycle, practice types, feedback, assessment, accessibility/differentiation and time assumptions.
- Traceability table separating verified official objectives from internal design statements.
- Rights/originality, accessibility, age/cultural suitability and release-gate status.
- Confidence rating, unresolved questions, and one recommended next step.

## 6. Prohibited shortcuts

Do not ask the founder again for school-account access. Do not wait indefinitely for gated sources. Do not invent official mapping. Do not treat an independent platform's sequence as an official syllabus. Do not copy proprietary resources into the platform. Do not describe a public-framework-aligned guide as officially mapped, endorsed, exhaustive or release-ready unless the relevant evidence and approvals actually support that claim.
