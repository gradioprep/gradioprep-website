# Diagnostic Assessment, Evaluation Analytics and Remedial Course Standard

**Version:** 1.0  
**Approved:** 31 October 2026  
**Status:** Binding process standard for the diagnostic-first learning-readiness layer.  
**Applies to:** Each curriculum/grade/stage cycle only when selected by the Founder / project owner.  
**Related records:** `docs/PROJECT_MASTER_BRIEF.md`, `docs/DIAGNOSTIC_READINESS_CYCLE_REGISTER.md`, `docs/ENGLISH_STAGE_1_ROADMAP.md`, `docs/ENGLISH_CURRICULUM_GUIDES.md`, `docs/TEACHER_PACK_AND_FEEDBACK_STANDARD.md`, `docs/CONTENT_ORIGINALITY_AND_RIGHTS_PROTOCOL.md` and `docs/DESIGN_SPEC.md`.

## 1. Purpose and governing decision

The diagnostic, evaluation report with analytics, and remedial course form one **pre-curriculum learning-readiness layer**. It identifies a learner's current underlying skills, explains the evidence, prioritises improvement and teaches the required skills before the learner is exposed to the selected curriculum's units.

This is not a curriculum-unit test, mock examination, official board assessment or replacement for the existing curriculum. It assesses transferable and prerequisite skills that help students learn, interpret, reason, communicate and perform successfully within the selected course.

Complete and validate all three features for one selected level before starting the next diagnostic cycle or any further curriculum content.

## 2. Binding order and content pause

The current order is defined only in `docs/DIAGNOSTIC_READINESS_CYCLE_REGISTER.md`:

Existing content, teacher packs, research, approvals, open PRs and outstanding rights/accessibility/release gates are preserved exactly as recorded. They are deferred, not cancelled, approved by implication or released. After these cycles, resume curriculum content only in the order the founder specifies, using the existing research, authoring, teacher-pack, rights, academic, accessibility, QA and release processes.

## 3. Scope lock for each selected level

Before provider research or item authoring, record the requested school-grade label, actual programme/course, school year/stage, learner age range, language route, applicable official framework and edition/session where identifiable, and any local/abbreviated implementation that affects placement. State what is verified, provisional or unresolved.

A school-grade label is not an official curriculum stage. Do not silently turn uncertain mapping into an official course-code or objective claim. If the exact placement cannot be confirmed, keep the requested grade label and record the limitation; do not silently substitute another stage.

## 4. Research: the five best diagnostic/evaluation providers for the selected level

Conduct a separate, current, evidence-led review for each selected level. Rank the **five providers/products that best demonstrate diagnostic assessment and skill-level evaluation for that specific learner age, grade and context**. This is a diagnostic-product landscape audit, not the existing curriculum-content Top Five audit. Do not simply select five popular general-purpose EdTech brands or reuse one cycle's ranking for another.

Consider diagnostic assessment specialists, literacy/reading/writing assessment products, adaptive assessment providers and age-/curriculum-relevant learning platforms. Official IB/Cambridge sources must be checked separately for programme context; they do not count among the five independent providers unless they offer a relevant, inspectable diagnostic product.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Diagnostic quality, construct validity and skill/sub-skill granularity | 25% |
| Fit to selected learner age, grade and context | 20% |
| Evaluation-report depth, analytics accuracy and interpretability | 20% |
| Actionable remediation and next-step linkage | 15% |

| Evidence transparency, currency, access, privacy and practical suitability | 10% |

For each provider record the exact product, intended ages/levels, skill domains, assessment model, public sample evidence, report fields and visuals, confidence/benchmark claims, remediation features, accessibility, pricing/access limits where verifiable, privacy/data practices, source dates and limitations. Distinguish observed capabilities from marketing claims. Do not infer paywalled functions, unpublished validity, hidden reports or unverified prices. Cite primary product documentation and independent evidence where available. Explain why each provider ranks in the Top Five and what GradiPrep can learn. Do not copy proprietary questions, report wording, visual identity or restricted materials.

## 5. Diagnostic blueprint and skill measurement

The assessment measures the **underlying skills and prerequisites** needed to learn successfully in the selected English curriculum, not questions copied from its units, guide or past papers.

The detailed skill taxonomy, sub-skills, age-level expectations, item formats, number of observations and scoring rules are cycle-specific deliverables. Finalise them after the cycle's research and official-context check; do not assume identical taxonomies or thresholds across curricula.

Candidate English skill families to investigate include comprehension, inference, evidence selection, interpretation, vocabulary and language in context, text structure, authorial/language choices, sentence control, grammar and punctuation, paragraph development, cohesion, writing organisation, reasoning, editing and purposeful communication. Include listening, speaking, viewing or presenting when relevant to the selected course, feasible in the product and supported by valid evidence. These are candidates, not a final approved blueprint.

Every item must have a stable ID/version, construct and skill tags, intended demand, response format, accepted response, scoring rule, misconception/error interpretation and teacher-pack link. Use multiple items or complementary evidence for important skill judgements. One wrong answer must not automatically establish a persistent gap. Where evidence is insufficient or inconsistent, report low confidence or recommend a further probe instead of presenting false precision.

Balance item types and performance evidence. Avoid allowing reading load, unfamiliar cultural references, speed, device fluency or language background to distort the intended skill unless that demand is deliberately being assessed. Record accessibility supports and accommodation assumptions. Keep the experience age-appropriate and non-punitive.

## 6. Evaluation report and analytics

The report is a central learning product, not a score sheet. It must translate diagnostic evidence into an accurate, understandable and actionable profile. Include:

- A concise learning-readiness overview and an explanation of what results do and do not mean.
- Skill/sub-skill performance with plain-language definitions.
- Evidence from actual learner responses and the task types used.
- Demonstrated strengths and areas for development.
- A confidence/evidence indicator for each judgement, including insufficient evidence where appropriate.
- Remediation priority and the reason for it.
- Exact links from each priority area to five-day remedial tasks.
- Clear next steps and a post-remediation comparison after valid reassessment.

Use readable horizontal bars for skill comparisons; radar charts only where a small set of comparable dimensions is interpretable; circle/donut charts only where categories form a meaningful whole and denominators are clear; and line graphs only when at least two valid measurements over time exist. Never manufacture a trend from one diagnostic, use decorative charts or overstate precision. Keep tables and dense data blocks to a minimum. Provide short explanations beside charts, accessible text equivalents, direct labels and honest scales. Do not show percentiles, grade predictions or population comparisons without a current, appropriately matched and documented norming basis.

## 7. Remediation priority levels and learner-facing language

Assign each improvement area a **Remediation Priority 5–1**:

- **Priority 5:** highest priority; a foundational or consequential skill requiring attention first.
- **Priority 4:** very high priority; a substantial barrier to successful independent work.
- **Priority 3:** important development area with a noticeable effect on performance.
- **Priority 2:** an area requiring targeted strengthening and practice.
- **Priority 1:** broadly satisfactory, with refinement still beneficial.

These are instructional routing priorities, not school/examination grades, permanent ability labels or judgements of learner worth. Student-facing language must remain respectful and strengths-based: use “focus area”, “next skill to strengthen” and “recommended practice”, not “weak”. Priority is based on evidence and likely effect on future learning, not simply the percentage of incorrect answers. Specify and human-review the assignment method and thresholds for each cycle. Record uncertainty instead of forcing every result into a confident category.

## 8. Five-day personalised remedial course

Each learner receives a plan based on their own diagnostic profile. The course is **five sessions of 60 minutes each**. Every identified improvement area must map to at least one specific teaching/practice task. If all needs cannot reasonably be addressed in five hours, state what remains and recommend further support rather than promise mastery.

Follow the same approved unit structure and teacher-pack standards as existing curriculum units. Each day includes clear objectives, the explicit purpose of every task, teaching/explanation, worked model or think-aloud where appropriate, guided practice, independent application/transfer, formative feedback and an exit check/reflection. Include enough meaningful questions and activities to use the full hour without padding.

Suggested 60-minute pattern: 5 minutes retrieval/recall, 10 minutes explicit teaching, 10 minutes worked example/model, 20 minutes guided practice, 10 minutes independent transfer and 5 minutes exit check/reflection. Adapt where the skill or age group requires, but show a time allocation totalling 60 minutes each day.

Teach and practise in descending priority, starting at Priority 5 and moving through 4, 3, 2 and 1. Do not create an artificial Priority 5 task where the learner has no such area. Priorities may recur across days for explicit instruction and spaced practice. Questions should progress from supported to increasingly independent work.

## 9. Remediation outcome check

Include a post-course check using fresh or equivalent items that measure the same target skills without simply repeating the original questions. Compare results only for genuinely comparable constructs and scoring rules. Report observed change, remaining support needs and limits on interpretation. A short-term score change alone does not prove durable learning or mastery.

## 10. Teacher/editor materials and human validation

Keep diagnostic keys, scoring rules, construct maps, exemplars, misconception interpretations, priority-assignment rules and private evaluation logic in a separate teacher/editor pack. The learner bundle must not expose hidden answers or private criteria before the designed reveal point.

For every diagnostic item, the pack explains how a response provides evidence of the intended skill. For every remedial activity, it records purpose, success evidence, accepted responses, tailored feedback and next step. AI may assist drafting but is not the academic validator. The Founder / project owner remains the human academic reviewer unless the founder changes this; do not ask redundantly who is reviewing.

Apply `docs/TEACHER_PACK_AND_FEEDBACK_STANDARD.md` v1.3 and the existing source, passage, image, territory, accessibility, academic and release rules.

## 11. Design, accessibility and architecture guardrails

Use the approved design system. Reports and analytics use a predominantly white/cool-white background, clear high-contrast text, restrained aubergine/dusty-blue emphasis, subtle lilac/blue-grey support surfaces and muted amber only for constructive next steps. No black/dark dashboard theme, global typography change, palette-token change or page-architecture redesign.

Charts must be understandable at narrow widths and at 200%/400% zoom, with keyboard/screen-reader access and a text equivalent. Do not rely on colour alone.

Reuse the current React/TypeScript application structure, existing components, teacher-pack separation, navigation and QA patterns. Complete an architecture-feasibility check before implementation. The current prototype lacks authentication, a database and persistent learner records. Do not claim saved cross-session reports or longitudinal analytics unless implemented and verified. Do not add a database, authentication, AI/API endpoint, paid service, new platform or architecture redesign without separate explicit approval. Report limitations rather than silently adding infrastructure.

Use synthetic test records and fictional answers in development/hosted previews. Do not place real student data in a public or unauthorised preview. Follow privacy/security controls before collecting or storing learner data.

## 12. Completion gates for each cycle

A cycle is complete only when scope/official context are checked; its independent Top Five is ranked and cited; skill taxonomy, test blueprint, scoring and priority rules are approved; diagnostic items and teacher/editor rationales are human-validated; the diagnostic works and scoring/accessibility are tested; report calculations, confidence, priorities and chart labels are verified; the five-day plan contains five 60-minute sessions and maps every improvement area; the fresh outcome check is validated; integration, privacy, responsive, keyboard/screen-reader, manual accessibility and browser QA pass; existing rights/originality and age/cultural suitability checks pass; the full student journey is demonstrated; and the Founder / project owner explicitly approves the exact cycle.

Drafting or passing automated tests alone does not complete or release a cycle. No next cycle or curriculum-content queue begins automatically. No merge, production deployment or release occurs without separate explicit approval.
