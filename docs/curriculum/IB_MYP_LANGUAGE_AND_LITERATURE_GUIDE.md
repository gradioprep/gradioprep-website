## 4. Year 1 pathway intent and progression

**Pathway ID:** ib-myp-lal-y1  
**Audience:** Beginning MYP Language and Literature learners; approximately lower-secondary age, without assuming school grade and language proficiency are identical.  
**Coverage statement:** Starter pathway built from public MYP framework information, not a complete syllabus.

### End-of-pathway learner outcomes — platform design decisions

By the end of the three planned units, a learner should increasingly be able to:
1. Read and view short literary or non-literary texts with purpose and identify main ideas, perspective and meaningful details.
2. Support an interpretation with accurate textual evidence rather than broad assertion.
3. Explain how a writer/creator choice influences meaning or audience response.
4. Adapt a short written or spoken text to a stated audience, purpose and form.
5. Compare how two texts or representations communicate different perspectives, including how context can affect meaning.
6. Listen to, respond to and build on another interpretation; present a coherent idea orally or through a suitable multimodal form.
7. Reflect on one specific strength and choose a next step from evidence in the response.

These are original internal outcomes inferred from the broad public framework; they are not quotations or official Year 1 objective/strand statements.

### Progression model

Start with noticing and evidence; move to purposeful communication; finish by integrating evidence, perspective and context across texts/modes. Each unit uses entry check → explicit model → guided practice → independent transfer → feedback → revision → exit evidence. Increase challenge through less scaffolding, stronger evidence selection, explanation of effect and independence—not vocabulary complexity alone.

| 2 | **Purpose, audience and voice** — examine how language and form serve an audience and purpose | Reading, listening, speaking, writing | Audience-aware response in a stated form, plus an explanation of deliberate language/register choices | Planned; platform design decision |

## 6. Unit planning sheets

### Unit 1 — How writers shape meaning

**Purpose:** Develop the habit of moving from observation to evidence to explanation.  
**Entry check:** Provide a short original or rights-cleared paragraph/visual-text combination. Ask the learner to identify the main idea, select one meaningful detail and explain one effect. Use this to select support, not label the learner.  
**Model:** Demonstrate how to notice a meaningful choice (word, image, contrast, ordering, sentence pattern or other text feature), point to evidence and explain a plausible effect in context. Do not reward feature-spotting without explanation.  
**Guided practice:** Identify a detail; choose the strongest evidence; distinguish observation from interpretation; improve an explanation by connecting evidence to effect.  
**Independent transfer:** Use a fresh text and ask the learner to explain how a choice shapes meaning.  
**Feedback loop:** One evidence-based strength, one priority improvement, a revision opportunity and an exit check using a different example.  
**Success evidence:** Relevant interpretation; specific and accurate evidence; explanation linked to the claim; clear communication.  
**Content note:** The existing preview can be considered for this slot after a fresh mapping, source/rights and accessibility review. Do not attach official objective codes until directly verified.

### Unit 2 — Purpose, audience and voice

**Purpose:** Develop awareness that communication choices should fit audience, purpose and form.  
**Entry check:** Compare two short original texts on a shared subject written for different audiences/purposes. Ask what each seeks to achieve and which details support that reading.  
**Model:** Annotate vocabulary, register, structure, tone and selection of detail. Model planning with audience, purpose and intended effect in mind.  
**Guided practice:** Rework a short passage for a new audience; choose between alternative openings; explain why a register or structural choice fits.  
**Independent transfer:** Compose an original short response in a specified form and explain two deliberate choices.  
**Feedback loop:** Review purpose fulfilment, audience fit, organization and clarity; revise one meaningful choice and compare versions.  
**Success evidence:** Clear purpose; appropriate register/form; organized ideas; reasons for choices grounded in the text.

### Unit 3 — Perspectives across texts

**Purpose:** Build an evidence-led comparison between two representations and communicate a reasoned interpretation.  
**Entry check:** Provide two short original, age-appropriate texts about a shared topic from different viewpoints. Ask the learner to notice one similarity and one difference.  
**Model:** Demonstrate the distinction between topic similarity and perspective; select comparable evidence; consider context without unsupported assumptions; explain how form/language contributes to a difference.  
**Guided practice:** Sort evidence by text; use a comparison grid; turn a side-by-side list into an analytical statement; rehearse a 60–90 second explanation.  
**Independent transfer:** Produce a short comparison and share its central claim in a brief oral or multimodal presentation.  
**Feedback loop:** Review evidence accuracy, comparison quality, reasoning about perspective/context and presentation clarity; revise a claim or evidence choice.  
**Success evidence:** The comparison uses both texts; it explains rather than merely lists similarities/differences; the presentation has a clear idea; reflection selects a justified next step.  
**Inclusion note:** Use varied voices and perspectives without presenting one text as representative of an entire culture or group.

## 7. Internal formative rubric

The following is **our platform rubric**, not an official IB criterion/achievement-level descriptor. Use it for low-stakes feedback only. Score each relevant dimension 0–3: 0 = not yet evidenced; 1 = emerging with substantial support; 2 = generally successful with gaps; 3 = clear and independent for this task. Add an evidence note and next action; do not convert a single task into a high-stakes mastery label.

| Internal dimension | What to look for | Next action if weak |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning and interpretation | Relevant claim/main idea grounded in the text/task | Revisit the task; highlight details that support or challenge the first idea |
| Evidence selection | Specific, accurate, relevant evidence | Practise selecting one strong detail and explaining why it fits |
| Reasoning about choices/perspective | Explains how evidence supports a claim or how a choice affects meaning/audience | Use claim → evidence → explanation/effect; remove unsupported statements |
| Communication and organization | Clear, logical and appropriate to purpose/audience | Plan a through-line, use precise transitions and revise for the reader/listener |
| Language and mode choices | Word choice, register and written/oral/visual form serve the task | Compare alternatives and justify the stronger choice |

Select only dimensions relevant to the task. When speaking, listening, viewing or presenting is intended, gather direct evidence rather than infer performance from writing alone.

## 8. Diagnostic and next-step logic

- **Start:** Low-stakes task on meaning, evidence and explanation; use learner confidence/prior experience as context, not scores.
- **Practice:** Worked model, short focused practice, gradual reduction of scaffolding, fresh-text transfer and revision.
- **If evidence selection is weak:** assign evidence-selection practice.
- **If evidence is accurate but explanation is weak:** practise choice/effect reasoning.
- **If reasoning is sound but communication is unclear:** practise organization, register and expression.
- **Exit check:** use a fresh item of comparable demand; do not mark success solely because a learner completes a step or sees an answer.
- **Data limitation:** the current preview retains responses only in temporary state. Do not imply progress is saved or graded until secure persistence and authorization exist.

## 9. Text selection, inclusion and rights

- Prefer original, rights-cleared texts and visuals. Keep a rights ledger for any quote/adaptation with source, extent, permission/licence basis and permitted use.
- Do not reproduce full IB/Cambridge papers, protected text inserts, paid book pages or third-party model answers. Link to official samples and create original items using verified task patterns.
- Include diverse literary/non-literary forms, perspectives and media over the year; review cultural framing and sensitive content.
- Provide readable layout, keyboard access, clear instructions, sufficient contrast and vocabulary support without removing the skill assessed.
- Offer support/core/stretch routes with the intended goal transparent; differentiate through evidence quality, inference and independence, not simply longer text.
- Complete separate accessibility and age-suitability reviews before any release claim.

## Part B — Year 2 chapter

**Pathway ID:** ib-myp-lal-y2  
**Coverage status:** Public-framework-aligned design; exact official Year 2 objective/strand mapping is not claimed.  
**Progression premise:** Build on Year 1's evidence-based explanations and purposeful communication by requiring more independent interpretation, more precise evidence selection and clearer explanation of how context/audience affects meaning. This is a platform design decision, not an official IB year descriptor.

### End-of-year learning aims (platform decisions)

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- move from straightforward inference to an interpretation supported by more than one detail;
- discuss how language and structural choices work together to shape meaning;
- plan and revise an original text for a clear purpose and audience;
- listen and respond constructively to alternative readings;
- compare texts using a meaningful shared lens rather than a list;
- reflect on the effectiveness of their own evidence and communication choices.

### Proposed sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **From inference to interpretation** | A short evidence-supported interpretation with two relevant details and an explanation of how they work together |
| 2 | **Choice, audience and voice** | A purposeful original response plus rationale explaining selected register, structure or language choices |
| 3 | **Compare form and viewpoint** | A supported comparison of two texts or representations, including how different choices shape a reader’s understanding |

### Teaching and assessment design

Start each unit with a short diagnostic; model the reasoning process; provide guided practice; fade scaffolds; transfer to fresh text; and allow revision. The internal platform rubric should focus on interpretation, evidence selection, reasoning, communication and the specific mode assessed. The end-of-year check should combine a reading/viewing response, an original communication task, and direct evidence of discussion/listening or presentation. Do not infer performance in one mode from writing alone.

**Readiness for Year 3:** A learner can select relevant evidence independently, explain rather than just identify choices, plan a coherent response, and compare perspectives with a supported line of reasoning. This is an internal readiness recommendation and not an official MYP progression statement.

## Part C — Year 3 chapter

**Pathway ID:** ib-myp-lal-y3  
**Coverage status:** Public-framework-aligned design; official Year 3 objectives/strands and criterion descriptors remain unverified.  
**Progression premise:** Move from supported interpretation to more sustained analysis, explicit reasoning about perspective/context, and more deliberate control of structure in the learner’s own work.

### End-of-year learning aims (platform decisions)

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- develop an analytical line of thought across multiple paragraphs or modes;
- select evidence for a purpose and explain its significance;
- consider perspective and context while distinguishing textual evidence from assumption;
- use structure, language, form and mode deliberately in their own communication;
- compare representations by analysing choices rather than only topic;
- present a supported interpretation and respond thoughtfully to questions.

### Proposed sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Structure, perspective and effect** | An analysis showing how selected language/structural choices build a perspective across a text |
| 2 | **Build a sustained analytical response** | A planned multi-paragraph response with a clear claim, evidence sequence, reasoning and revision |
| 3 | **Compare representations and context** | A comparative response using relevant evidence from two texts, with justified claims about viewpoint and context |

### Teaching and assessment design

Model the difference between an observation, a claim and an analytical explanation. Include short tasks on building an argument through paragraphs, followed by an independent response to a fresh text. For oral/viewing/presenting tasks, teach source selection, multimodal design and audience awareness; assess the communication mode directly. Use internal platform criteria only, and avoid attaching unverified MYP criterion names, strands or achievement-level bands.

**Readiness for Year 4:** A learner can sustain an analytical thread, select and explain evidence with limited prompting, make a justified comparison and revise work against explicit criteria. This is an internal readiness recommendation.

## Part D — Year 4 chapter

**Pathway ID:** ib-myp-lal-y4  
**Coverage status:** Public-framework-aligned design; official Year 4 objective/strand mapping is not claimed.  
**Progression premise:** Increase complexity and independence through layered interpretation, alternative readings, more nuanced context and greater control of text form and audience.

### End-of-year learning aims (platform decisions)

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- analyse how several choices interact to shape meaning or reader response;
- use a text’s context to enrich but not replace textual analysis;
- compare nuanced perspectives and acknowledge ambiguity where the evidence supports it;
- create a sustained response with purposeful organization and suitable register;
- select an oral, written or multimodal form appropriate to the communication goal;
- evaluate the effectiveness of a draft and make substantial revisions.

### Proposed sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Layered meaning and deliberate craft** | Close analysis that connects multiple choices to a coherent interpretation |
| 2 | **Context, ambiguity and interpretation** | Two plausible readings evaluated against precise evidence and careful contextual reasoning |
| 3 | **Synthesis across genres and media** | A comparative or multimodal response synthesizing ideas across different text forms for a stated audience |

### Teaching and assessment design

Use complex but age-appropriate original texts with accessible vocabulary support where needed. Model how to manage uncertainty: distinguish what a text states, what it suggests, and what remains unproven. Introduce longer tasks by splitting them into a planning checkpoint, a draft, a feedback conference and a revised version. Make the feedback specific: one strength, one high-impact improvement and a reason for the next recommended practice.

**Readiness for Year 5:** A learner can independently analyse complex choices, build a sustained evidence-based interpretation, account for relevant context and revise a purposeful response.

## Part E — Year 5 chapter

**Pathway ID:** ib-myp-lal-y5  
**Coverage status:** Public-framework-aligned design; exact current Year 5 objective/strand mapping and applicable eAssessment requirements have not been verified from detailed public descriptors.  
**Progression premise:** Consolidate the public MYP Language and Literature skill domains and prepare learners for the actual pathway chosen by their school. Do not assume every learner sits an optional MYP eAssessment or that platform tasks reproduce the official on-screen exam.

### End-of-year learning aims (platform decisions)

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- independently analyse literary and non-literary texts using precise, relevant evidence;
- explain how language, structure, form, context and perspective shape meaning;
- create coherent, purposeful written, oral and/or multimodal communication;
- compare texts through a sustained line of reasoning;

### Proposed sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Independent analytical reading** | A timed but low-stakes analysis of an unfamiliar text, followed by revision and an error diagnosis |
| 2 | **Sustained response and comparative reasoning** | A well-planned response that integrates evidence, analysis, context and comparison where appropriate |
| 3 | **Integrated interpretation and communication** | A selected written/oral/multimodal task with direct evidence of interpretation, purposeful delivery and reflection |

The public IB states that specimen examinations are in the protected Programme Resource Centre. Do not use unauthorized “MYP past papers” from download websites as a standard. Do not market them as official specimen exams or guarantee examination outcomes.

**End-of-pathway readiness:** The learner shows independent interpretation, evidence selection, purposeful communication, comparative reasoning and revision across relevant modes. This is a platform-designed readiness profile, not an official IB grade.
