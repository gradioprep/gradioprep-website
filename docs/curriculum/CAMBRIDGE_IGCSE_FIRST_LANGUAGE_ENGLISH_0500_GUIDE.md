## 3. Verified high-level assessment architecture

The 2027–2029 official syllabus says candidates must use the full syllabus and updated specimen materials as the final authority for assessment content and structure. The current public specimen set shows the reworked paper structure:

### Paper 1 — Reading (2027–2029 structure)

The official Version 1 syllabus specifies a 2-hour, 80-mark paper with three texts and four compulsory questions carrying 20 marks each. The current question set includes:

- **Question 1:** comprehension work focused on understanding information from a text.
- **Question 2:** summary-related response, including a 5-mark question, with 15 reading marks and 5 writing marks.
- **Question 3:** shorter language/reading task with a 200–250-word response.
- **Question 4:** extended response to reading, 250–300 words, allocating 10 marks to reading and 10 to writing.

These descriptors summarise the current 2027–2029 specimen/syllabus structure. Before producing any task that claims to be a mock question, confirm the exact text source, instruction wording, marks, response mode and assessment objectives in the official specimen and mark scheme.

### Paper 2 — Directed Writing and Composition (2027–2029 structure)

The 2027–2029 specimen revises the directed-writing question into two parts, Question 1(a) and Question 1(b). Question 1(a) assesses reading objectives in a new form compared with previous question structures; Question 1(b) carries 10 reading marks and 25 writing marks. The remainder of the paper includes composition. The exact component/route, prompts, timings and marks must be read from the current official syllabus/specimen, not inferred from the high-level summary here.

### Important caveat

The above does not assert that every candidate takes the same route under every centre/option. Use the current syllabus route table to determine the candidate’s components. Do not build a commercial “full mock exam” or quote a predicted grade from a route that has not been confirmed.

## 4. Core skill model

Every task maps to the exact 0500 syllabus objectives only after the applicable official objective wording is verified. At the design level, organise learning around two connected skill families:

### Reading

- Identify and select explicit information and relevant detail.
- Infer meaning that is supported by the text.
- Understand the writer’s language, style, tone, structure, viewpoint and effect as required by the current assessment objectives.
- Select evidence that is precise and relevant to the question.
- Summarise selectively and accurately, avoiding repeated wording and unnecessary detail.
- Evaluate and explain choices or ideas where the current task demands it.

### Writing

- Communicate clearly for a defined purpose and audience.
- Use an appropriate form, register and tone.
- Shape and organize ideas into a coherent, purposeful structure.
- Use relevant details from the reading text accurately when directed writing requires it.
- Control vocabulary, sentence structure, grammar, spelling and punctuation so language choices support meaning.
- Review and revise for relevance, clarity, effect and accuracy.

These are an instructional model derived from the public syllabus and specimens, not a replacement for the official objective codes, grade descriptors or mark schemes.

## 5. Proposed two-phase delivery sequence

This is our suggested teaching sequence for learners taught over approximately two school years. It is not an official Cambridge split and may need to be adapted to school calendars, learner readiness, language background and the chosen exam series.

### Phase A — Foundations and controlled response

**Purpose:** Establish accurate reading, evidence selection and controlled writing before increasing exam-style time pressure.

Priority outcomes:
1. Read for explicit meaning and select precise evidence.
2. Make a reasonable inference and distinguish it from unsupported speculation.
3. Explain how a language/structural choice contributes to meaning using evidence.
4. Practise summary through selection and concise re-expression.
5. Plan and organize directed writing for a stated purpose/audience using relevant source information.
6. Build paragraphing, sentence control, grammar and vocabulary in context.
7. Establish a repeatable review process: check task requirements, evidence, structure, purpose and accuracy.

Suggested original practice progression:
- one-skill microtasks;
- scaffolded short responses;
- annotated examples;
- guided summary/planning;
- complete response with feedback;
- fresh transfer task with fewer supports.

### Phase B — Integration and current-format exam readiness

**Purpose:** Combine reading and writing skills, build independence and use the exact current paper structure once the candidate’s examination session and route are confirmed.

Priority outcomes:
1. Complete unseen-reading tasks with deliberate time allocation.
2. Select and integrate evidence efficiently.
3. Write a focused response to reading, balancing understanding and written expression according to the exact question.
4. Complete directed writing that uses source content purposefully without copying text indiscriminately.
5. Produce original compositions with controlled structure, audience, voice and detail.
6. Diagnose errors from specimen-based practice and revise a response.
7. Complete current-format practice sets and then full practice papers only after route and timing are checked.

### Phase-label rule

The two phases share the same 0500 syllabus where appropriate. They are not “0500 Year 1 syllabus” and “0500 Year 2 syllabus”. Every objective is mapped to the same applicable official syllabus edition; the distinction is recommended teaching progression and readiness.

## 6. Unit architecture (three initial slots)

The roadmap reserves three unit slots per pathway. These are platform design decisions and can be revised after objectives are verified.

| Unit | Proposed focus | Main practice evidence | Current-syllabus check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read accurately and support an inference** | Short comprehension and evidence-based responses using original texts | Map each prompt to the 2027–2029 official reading objective and matching specimen demand before using as a current-format item |
| 2 | **Summarise, explain and respond to reading** | Original summary task and response-to-reading task with feedback on selection, relevance and expression | Match response length, marks and reading/writing split to current official specimen and mark scheme |
| 3 | **Write for purpose, audience and effect** | Original directed-writing prompt plus original composition with task-specific feedback | Verify the prompt form, current task parts, route and mark allocation against Paper 2 specimen/syllabus |

For each unit, include entry check, worked example, guided practice, independent transfer, error-specific feedback, revision and exit check. The learner needs to know not just whether the answer was correct but which reading/writing move to improve next.

## 7. Assessment feedback and diagnostic routing

Use a platform learning rubric, not a substitute Cambridge mark scheme. Score only the dimensions actually required by the task:
- **Reading understanding:** factual accuracy, supported inference, relevant selection.
- **Evidence and explanation:** precision and how the evidence addresses the instruction.
- **Summary/selectivity:** relevance, concision and accurate re-expression when summary is required.
- **Purpose/audience/form:** clear fulfilment of the specific writing task.
- **Organization and effect:** logical sequencing, coherent development, effective paragraphing and purposeful choices.
- **Language control:** appropriate vocabulary, syntax, grammar, spelling and punctuation.

Next-step rules:
- Misread explicit meaning → practise locating and combining details.
- Unsupported inference → practise evidence selection and justification.
- Descriptive analysis → practise explaining how the selected evidence answers the question.
- Summary contains too much irrelevant detail → practise prioritizing and compressing meaning.
- Directed response does not use the source purposefully → plan around task role, audience and required content before drafting.
- Writing is disorganized → practise planning and paragraph sequence.
- Repeated language errors affect meaning → teach the identified error pattern in context and revise actual sentences.

Keep marks and rubric version separate from content version. A marked practice must retain the correct syllabus/session and mark scheme used. Do not present a platform rubric score as an official Cambridge grade.

## 8. Sample-paper selection and rights protocol

Before any past paper or specimen can shape a task, document:
1. 0500 code and exam-year syllabus version.
2. Official or third-party source, exact URL and date checked.
3. Specimen versus live past paper versus teacher-created practice.
4. Paper/component and question number, if being used for internal reference.
5. Current-syllabus comparison: text type, task instruction, marks, response length, time and objective.
6. Whether the paper is legally viewable and reusable or must only be linked.
7. Original task ID created for the platform and its academic reviewer.

The platform should create original texts and original questions. Do not copy or rehost Cambridge paper content, paid textbook pages or protected mark schemes. Direct users to the official Cambridge page for papers they are entitled to access. A third-party download or a “recent” upload is not proof of currentness or permission.
