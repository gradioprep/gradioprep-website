## Administration and evidence notes

The task uses one original prompt with an explicit audience, purpose and controlled form. It does not depend on a supplied passage, image, specialist knowledge or a prior curriculum unit. The learner develops an original scene from the shared situation below.

The proposed time is 15 minutes. A suggested allocation is 2 minutes to plan, 10 minutes to draft and 3 minutes to revise. The proposed response guidance is **100–150 words, ideal target 125 words**. The range and time are guidance for pilot review, not validated limits. Do not truncate, reject, disable or block a response that exceeds the ideal target or 150 words.

The planning record and revision trace are essential evidence. Retain the brief plan, initial draft, final text and one before/after revision example (or an equivalent concise revision note). Do not infer CW05 craft-and-revision evidence from the polished final text alone. The task does not ask for a reflective essay about writing; the revision note is a brief evidence record, separate from the narrative word count.

## Learner task — draft wording

### CW01–CW05 — Short narrative scene

During a school open afternoon, rain moves the outdoor book swap into a busy hall. One small corner beside the windows is left unused.

Write a **short realistic narrative scene** for readers aged **10–13** of the school magazine. Your purpose is to engage these readers and show how an unexpected change can help people see a shared space differently.

Make one character notice something others have overlooked and choose what to do. Keep one viewpoint consistent, and create an ending that grows naturally from the scene. Use dialogue, description and action where they help your purpose.

**Response guidance:** 100–150 words.
**Ideal target:** 125 words.
**Proposed task time:** 15 minutes (planning, drafting and revision included).

Before writing, make three brief plan notes:

1. Whose viewpoint will guide the scene?
2. What changes unexpectedly?
3. What choice or detail will shape the ending?

After drafting, make at least one purposeful edit to improve clarity, precision, coherence or effect. Record the original wording and the revised wording, with a brief note explaining the improvement. Keep the plan and revision record; they are part of the evidence for this task.

The word target is guidance only. A response above 150 words must remain editable and submit-able. The plan and revision note are not counted in the narrative word total.
