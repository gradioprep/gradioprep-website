## 2. Stimulus overview and construct fit

The report describes SolarSPELL, a solar-powered offline digital library that provides a WiFi hotspot and locally stored educational resources in communities with limited or no internet connectivity. It includes narration and interview perspectives from people involved in the project and discusses local needs and the limits of one-size-fits-all technology.

Potential evidence opportunities include the main proposal, explicit project details, an interviewee's concern about educational isolation, the report's audience and purpose, emphatic spoken wording, and the limits of generalising from one community's experience.

Source length is 4:10 against the current 10-minute Listening allocation. This leaves approximately 5:50 for instructions and five observations. Timing, replay allowance and cognitive load require pilot validation; no play/replay rule is fixed by this draft.

## 3. Provisional learner questions

### L01 — Central message and explicit information

**L01a.** Which statement best expresses the report's central message?

A. SolarSPELL is a solar-powered offline digital library designed to widen access to educational content in remote communities.
B. SolarSPELL connects remote communities to the full public internet through satellite service.
C. SolarSPELL replaces classroom teachers with online courses.
D. SolarSPELL supplies electricity to entire villages.

**L01b.** Select two details that the report gives about the SolarSPELL project.

A. The solar-powered unit creates a WiFi hotspot without needing an internet connection.
B. The Peace Corps has used about 50 SolarSPELL units.
C. Each unit depends on permanent power cables and a live internet connection.
D. The units are available only to university students in the United States.

### L02 — Inference and speaker viewpoint

Why does Alexis Cullen worry that communities without internet access may fall further behind? What wider concern about education and participation is she expressing?

**Response guidance:** 25–35 words. Ideal target: 30 words. Guidance is not a hard word limit.

### L03 — Audience, context and purpose

Who is the report's most likely audience, and what is its main purpose?

A. General listeners interested in education and technology; to explain an offline-learning initiative while considering its benefits and limits.
B. Engineers at the university; to provide a technical construction manual for the device.
C. Residents of Naviso; to ask them to purchase a SolarSPELL unit.
D. Peace Corps staff; to instruct them to stop using community learning programmes.

### L04 — Spoken language choice and emphasis

Gabriel Krieshok describes a universal approach as a “one size-fits-all solution” and says that it “never works”. Explain how this wording communicates his concern about using the same technology in different communities.

**Response guidance:** 25–35 words. Ideal target: 30 words. Guidance is not a hard word limit.

### L05 — Evaluate a conclusion

A listener concludes: “Because SolarSPELL has helped students in Vanuatu, the same technology will solve the lack of educational access in every remote community.” Is this conclusion fully supported, partly supported or exaggerated? Use evidence from the report and identify one limitation.

**Response guidance:** 35–45 words. Ideal target: 40 words. Guidance is not a hard word limit.
