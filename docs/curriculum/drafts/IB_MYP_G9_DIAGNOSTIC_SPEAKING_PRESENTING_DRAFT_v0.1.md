## Administration and evidence notes

The task uses one shared, familiar school-community situation. It does not require specialist knowledge, research, a slide deck, a visual asset or prior curriculum content. Learners may choose any practical proposal that addresses the scenario; no particular use of the space is preferred.

The approved planning envelope is 10 minutes:
- 2 minutes to prepare the outline card;
- up to 3 minutes for the presentation;
- up to 2 minutes for the standardised follow-up;
- 3 minutes for transition and assessor-note completion.

The card is a speaking aid, not a slide-design task or a separately scored written response. Spelling, handwriting, decorative design and drawing skill are not assessed. The assessor records observations in the restricted scoring sheet; no audio/video recording is assumed or retained. The timing allocation is a proposal for pilot validation, not a calibrated speed measure.

## Learner task — draft wording

### SP01–SP05 — A better shared space

Your school has one unused corner in a shared indoor area. The student council is collecting ideas about how it could be used.

Prepare a short presentation recommending **one practical use** for this corner. You will speak to members of the student council.

Your purpose is to explain why your idea would be useful to students with different needs or preferences and to help the council decide whether to try it.

In your presentation:
- explain your recommendation clearly;
- develop one or two reasons, with a relevant example of how the idea could work;
- consider one possible concern or a different preference; and
- finish with a clear recommendation.

You may propose any reasonable use of the space. You do not need to research the topic or use slides.

### Prepare your outline card

Use the card to make a brief plan. Include:
1. **Title:** a short title that signals your proposal.
2. **Opening:** your recommendation in one clear point.
3. **Development:** one or two reasons/examples in a logical order.
4. **Consideration:** one possible concern or different preference and your response.
5. **Closing:** the decision or next step you want the council to take.

You may add simple signposting words such as “first”, “for example”, “however” and “so I recommend”. Use the layout that makes your points easiest to follow. Short notes are enough; do not write a full speech.

### Present and respond

Present your proposal for up to 3 minutes. The assessor will then ask the same follow-up question to every learner:

**Follow-up:** “A student might prefer the corner to be used differently. What concern might they raise, and what would you say in response?”

Respond in your own words. You may refer to your outline card.

## Draft evidence crosswalk

| Observation | Evidence sought | Direct taxonomy |
|---|---|---|
| SP01 | A purposeful outline card with a clear title, a recognisable presentation structure, a logical point sequence and useful signposting/layout | B1, B5, C2 |
| SP02 | Spoken ideas progress logically, connect clearly and include enough relevant explanation or example to develop the recommendation | B2, B3, B4 |
| SP03 | Word choice, explanation and delivery are suited to the student-council audience and the purpose of proposing a practical shared-space use | C3 |
| SP04 | The learner communicates a relevant viewpoint, explains why it matters and responds thoughtfully to a different preference or concern | C4 |
| SP05 | Speech is intelligible and appropriately paced, with purposeful emphasis and interaction; posture, gesture or expression are considered only when they help communicate meaning | D5, D6 |

One prepared presentation and the follow-up provide evidence for the five observations; they are not five separate oral questions. Judge each dimension separately. Do not reward confidence, extroversion, accent conformity, eye contact as a cultural norm or disability-related speech characteristics as proxies for communication skill. Assess intelligibility and communicative effectiveness in context.
