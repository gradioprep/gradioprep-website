## 1. Learning-design summary

**Platform learning objective:** Read a short narrative for explicit meaning; draw a reasonable inference from textual evidence; select appropriate evidence; explain how a word or detail supports an interpretation.

**Public curriculum basis:** The official Cambridge Lower Secondary English (0861) page identifies reading, writing, speaking and listening as four skill areas, recommends fiction, poetry, playscripts and non-fiction across the stage, and supports an integrated experience/reflection/improvement approach. The public framework-support overview explicitly labels 7Ri.03 as an objective from the Curriculum Framework; this supports the limited explicit-information crosswalk only. Cambridge says the complete current framework and progression grid are provided through the support site, so the applicable file/version and inference/language-choice objective codes remain unverified. This is a platform-designed reading-focused unit, not a full official objective map or a complete Stage 7 course.

**Time:** Approximately 25–35 minutes, including reflection; allow longer where reading support or extended writing is needed.  
**Prerequisites:** Basic reading fluency sufficient to follow a short narrative; no prior knowledge of literary terminology required.  
**Materials:** Original passage, optional paper/highlighting or accessible digital equivalent, response fields.  
**Assessment use:** Low-stakes formative practice only; not a Cambridge Progression Test or Checkpoint mock.

## 2. Worked model and optional word support

The learner-facing worked model uses the warm-up mini-example to model a reasoning sequence without giving away the answers to the passage questions:

- **Stated clues:** the map's corners fluttered; a loose sheet of paper moved across the floor.
- **Inference:** air was probably moving through the room.
- **Reasoning:** two light objects move in ways consistent with air movement.
- **Boundary:** the text does not state that the room was cold or specify why air was moving.

This aligns with evidence-informed reading-comprehension guidance that recommends consciously modelling strategies before learners use them in guided and independent practice. See the public EEF references in the review record and source register.

Optional word support is learner-facing for *pane*, *hinge*, *rain gauge*, *paved patch* and *seed trays*. The learner version does not predefine *skittered* or *crept*, the two words explicitly analysed in the practice tasks. Learners may mark paper, make digital notes, or simply identify details in their response; paper annotation is not a prerequisite.

## 3. Answer guide and acceptable alternatives

### Warm-up — facts and inferences

Correct explicit statement: “The map's corners moved and a sheet of paper skated across the floor.” “Air was moving through the room” is a reasonable inference supported by both details, but it is not stated directly. “The room was cold” and “Dev was frightened” are not supported by those sentences alone.

### Guided practice A — find a fact

Expected answer: Jamil was meant to finish his notes on plant growth.

### Guided practice B — select evidence

Any two relevant details may receive credit, including:
- the loose greenhouse door was difficult to close and its hinge squealed;
- a gust sent leaves skittering across the tiles;
- Ms Chen had moved the seed trays away from the floor;
- the rain was stronger;
- a stream crossed the path;
- water gathered at the cracked stone and slipped around the edge.

For this selection-only task, do not require explanation unless the learner volunteers it; mark whether the two details are accurate and relevant.

### Guided practice C — make an inference

Strong interpretation: Jamil believes the seed trays may be at risk from the rain/water and decides to help move them to a safer place. Accept another plausible reading if supported by accurate textual evidence. Relevant clues include the growing water flow, Ms Chen moving trays away from the floor, room on the bench, and Jamil putting his notebook away to move the trays. Do not insist on one exact wording.

### Guided practice D — explain “skittered”

Strong answer: “Skittered” suggests quick, light, uneven or skimming movement. It makes the leaves seem easily pushed or scattered by the gust and helps the reader picture the wind affecting the tiled space. A response that only says “it is a verb” or “it means moved” is incomplete unless it explains the effect in context.

### Independent practice 1 — explicit information

Expected answer: The small rain gauge. Its new location is an open paved patch where it can collect rainfall rather than being sheltered.

### Independent practice 2 — evidence selection

Any two accurate details suggesting stronger rain/wind or increasing water flow are acceptable. The learner should copy or identify enough of each detail to make it identifiable.

### Independent practice 3 — supported inference

Expected: Jamil may be trying to protect the seed trays or help Ms Chen because the worsening weather/water could affect the plants. The response must use at least two appropriate clues and explain how they support the idea. Accept other reasonable interpretations that fit the passage.

### Independent practice 4 — explain “crept”

Strong answer: “Crept” presents the water as moving slowly and almost quietly along the path, so the change first appears gradual. The next clauses show it collecting and then slipping around the edge, suggesting the water is spreading. Accept an alternative supported by the sequence.

### Independent practice 5 — evaluate “Jamil stops writing because he is bored”

Expected: This is not well supported. The passage states that he watches the garden, notices changing weather, sets the gauge and then moves the trays. His actions give stronger evidence of responding to the conditions than of boredom. A learner may mention boredom as a possibility but must acknowledge that the passage does not provide direct evidence for it.

### Independent practice 6 — extended response (80–100 words)

Look for:
- a clear account of how Jamil's focus changes;
- at least two accurate, precise details;
- explanation of how the details support the interpretation;
- coherent organization and readable expression.

A strong response might connect the early observation of the water/gauge with the later decision to move the trays after the weather strengthens. Do not require the exact model answer or treat a defensible alternative as wrong.

### Exit check

Reasonable inference: Lina expects rain or changing weather and is packing up to keep the blanket or picnic items dry. Supporting details include the darkening sky and the cup rolling along the platform (which may suggest wind). A complete answer names the inference, points to two clues and explains how they fit. If a learner notices only one clue, ask what the second clue adds.

## 4. Suggested formative scoring (platform rubric only)

This is an internal teaching aid, not a Cambridge mark scheme. It is deliberately a **15-point formative profile**, not a grade or proxy for Cambridge assessment.

| Item | Points | What to look for |
|---|---:|---|
| Independent practice 1 — explicit retrieval | 0–1 | 1 for identifying the small rain gauge; otherwise 0. |
| Independent practice 2 — evidence selection | 0–2 | 1 point for each distinct, accurate, relevant detail; max 2. Do not require an explanation for this selection-only prompt. |
| Independent practice 3 — inference | 0–2 | 0 for no plausible inference or unsupported assertion; 1 for a plausible inference with partial evidence or an unclear link; 2 for a reasonable inference, two relevant clues and a clear link between clues and inference. |
| Independent practice 4 — language in context | 0–2 | 0 for no relevant interpretation; 1 for a basic paraphrase or effect with little contextual reasoning; 2 for a plausible explanation of the effect of “crept” in this sentence/sequence. |
| Independent practice 5 — evaluate an interpretation | 0–2 | 0 for an unsupported judgement; 1 for a judgement with one relevant detail or an alternative interpretation without clear reasoning; 2 for a supported judgement explaining how accurate evidence does or does not support the claim. |
| Extended response 6 | 0–6 | Interpretation (0–2); accurate, relevant evidence (0–2); reasoning and coherent communication (0–2). |

**Maximum: 15 points** (item 1: 1; items 2–5: 8; item 6: 6). The exit check and reflection are unscored diagnostic activities. Accept defensible alternatives when supported by the text. Do not convert these formative points into an official stage grade or predicted score. Record evidence and one next action.

## 5. Common errors and next-step recommendations

| Learner response pattern | Likely learning gap | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Gives a general opinion without answering the question | Task focus / explicit understanding | Highlight task keywords and locate a sentence that directly answers them |
| Gives an inference without textual clues | Evidence selection | Ask for two clues, then ask how they connect |
| Copies a sentence but does not say why it matters | Explanation | Add “This supports my idea because…” and connect the detail to the claim |
| Names a word but only repeats its dictionary meaning | Analysis of language in context | Compare the word's effect with a neutral alternative and use the surrounding sentence |
| Claims a character is angry, bored or frightened without support | Overinterpretation | Sort observations, reasonable inferences and unsupported guesses |
| Writes a clear answer but lacks organization | Communication | Use claim → evidence → explanation, then remove scaffolds as independence improves |

## 6. Teacher notes and differentiation

- **Support:** invite learners to use the word-help box, model one evidence chain aloud, allow read-aloud or text-to-speech where available, and provide the sentence frame.
- **Core:** use the worked model once; then remove the frame for independent questions and require two clues for an inference.
- **Stretch:** ask learners to compare two interpretations and judge which is better supported, or analyse a second word choice.
- **Vocabulary:** explicitly teach vocabulary needed to access the passage without pre-solving the targeted “skittered” and “crept” analysis.
- **Language accessibility:** avoid treating vocabulary familiarity as the only measure of reading skill; support access without supplying the inference.
- **Mode:** this unit directly assesses reading and written explanation. It does not claim to assess the full speaking/listening strand; that is scheduled in Unit 3 of the proposed Stage 7 guide.
