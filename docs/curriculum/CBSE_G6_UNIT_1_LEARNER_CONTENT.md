## Why are we learning this?

In this short story, follow the events and notice how a character moves from a quick guess to a better-supported conclusion. Then practise using words that describe location.

## Read the passage

### The Turning Sign

Each morning, the animals followed a narrow trail from the hill to a pool. At a fork stood a wooden arrow showing the path that stayed dry after rain. One evening, a gust turned the arrow toward the steep bank. Lali the langur noticed hoofprints near the post and guessed that the deer had pushed it. Naren the tortoise asked her to examine the ground before deciding. Beside the post, they found a fresh scrape in the soil. It continued down the slope to a loose wooden wedge lying behind a broad root. A fallen branch had knocked the wedge from its slot, leaving the arrow free to turn in the wind. Lali set the wedge back and pointed the arrow toward the safe path. She apologised to the deer. The animals placed two flat stones beside the post, so anyone could see where its base belonged. Naren reminded them, “Hoofprints show that a deer passed here; they do not explain who moved the sign.”

## 1. Find a detail

**Which detail helps explain why the arrow turned?**

- A. The deer had walked near the post.
- B. A loose wooden wedge lay behind a broad root.
- C. The arrow showed the route that stayed dry after rain.
- D. The animals placed two flat stones beside the post.

Write the detail you selected in your own words.

## 2. Explain an inference

Why does Lali apologise to the deer? Use one detail from the passage to support your explanation. Remember to distinguish what the hoofprints show from what they do not prove.

## 3. Put events in order

Number these events from 1 to 4.

- The wooden wedge is found behind a broad root.
- Lali notices hoofprints and makes a guess.
- Lali apologises to the deer and resets the arrow.
- A scrape in the soil leads down the slope to the loose wedge.

## 4. Location words in context

Complete each sentence using a suitable word from this list: **at · near · down · behind · beside**

1. The wooden arrow stood ______ a fork in the trail.
2. The hoofprints were ______ the post.
3. The scrape continued ______ the slope.
4. The loose wedge lay ______ a broad root.
5. The flat stones were placed ______ the post.

Use each word once.

## 5. Exit task: retell the solution

Write **50–70 words** explaining how the animals found out why the arrow turned and what Lali learned. Include:
- at least one event-order word, such as *first, next, then* or *finally*;
- at least two location prepositions from the passage;
- one detail explaining why Lali changed her mind.

There may be more than one reasonable explanation, but it must fit the passage.
