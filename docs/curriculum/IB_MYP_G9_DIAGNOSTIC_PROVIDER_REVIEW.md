## 1. Executive finding

The strongest current products for informing GradiPrep's Grade 9 / MYP Year 4 diagnostic design are:

1. **IXL LevelUp Diagnostic for ELA** — strongest overall combination of Grade 9-specific psychometric reporting, Reading plus Writing & Language diagnostics, strand-level reporting and direct skill recommendations.
2. **Edmentum Exact Path Diagnostic Assessment** — strong grade 9–12 fit, independent NCII screening-tool review, domain-level placement, individualized learning paths and accessible administration.
3. **NWEA MAP Growth Reading + Language Usage** — strongest mature adaptive measurement and growth/psychometric evidence, with useful instructional-area reporting; remediation must be supplied separately.
4. **Lexia PowerUp Literacy** — strong grades 6–12 literacy intervention and progress monitoring, with external research evidence; narrower than a complete language-and-literature diagnostic.
5. **GL Assessment Progress Test in English (PTE)** — useful independently reviewed attainment measure for reading comprehension, authorial technique, inference, grammar and punctuation; age/norm and remediation limitations reduce its fit.

**No provider is a complete MYP Language and Literature diagnostic.** The IB subject framework names six skill areas—listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing and presenting—and expects engagement with varied literary/non-literary texts, contexts, audiences, purposes and language/literary devices. The shortlisted products principally assess reading and written-language constructs. GradiPrep must not mistake their domains or scores for full MYP coverage, nor copy their proprietary questions, scoring models or content.

## 4. Provider findings

### 1. IXL LevelUp Diagnostic for ELA — 92/100

**Age/grade fit and constructs.** The December 2025 technical manual covers PK–12 Reading and Grades 2–12 Writing & Language. Reading includes literary and informational texts; Writing & Language covers writing and language. This includes Grade 9 directly and is substantially broader than a reading-only screener.

**Assessment and evidence.** Computer-adaptive delivery adjusts item difficulty to responses. The manual reports Grade 9 internal-consistency analysis samples of 55,851 for Reading and 41,440 for Writing & Language. Grade 9 internal consistency is reported as .94 for both components. Grade 9 classification accuracy/consistency is .928/.898 for Reading and .916/.883 for Writing & Language. For Grades 9–12, 30-day test-retest ICCs are described as good (above .60), rather than excellent; do not overstate these as perfect stability.

**Reports/remediation.** Public documentation shows diagnostic levels by strand, a Diagnostic Strand Analysis, student-level recommendations, an action plan, progress/growth reporting and teacher reports. The technical manual describes personalized recommendations based on the learner's diagnostic profile.

**Limitations.** The standards architecture is based on US state/CCSS-style ELA strands, not IB MYP criteria or the six MYP skill areas. No listening, speaking, viewing or presenting diagnostic is evidenced in the reviewed ELA diagnostic. The timed item windows and combined test duration need careful review before any use with MYP learners. Published technical evidence is largely provider-authored; external validation claims and norm transfer to an international MYP cohort must be treated cautiously. It is a benchmark/reference product, not a ready-made MYP blueprint.

**GradiPrep learning:** strong example for adaptive item selection, evidence at both reading and writing/language strand level, explicit confidence/measurement reporting, personalized skill recommendations and linking diagnostic findings to practice. Do not copy its items, proprietary skill content or interface.

Sources:
- [IXL LevelUp Diagnostic for ELA Technical Manual (December 2025)](https://www.ixl.com/materials/IXL_LevelUp_Diagnostic_for_ELA_Technical_Manual.pdf).
- [IXL Analytics](https://ixl.college/analytics) — diagnostic strand analysis, student recommendations and growth reports.
- [IXL Diagnostic Hub](https://www.ixl.com/diagnostic-hub) — technical guides and research index.

### 2. Edmentum Exact Path Diagnostic Assessment — 91/100

**Age/grade fit and constructs.** The independent National Center on Intensive Intervention (NCII) profile lists the Reading diagnostic for Grades 1–11, including Grade 9, and ages 5–18. It is a computer-adaptive reading assessment, with domain-level placement and a linked individualized learning path.

**Assessment and evidence.** NCII describes real-time domain and overall placement, growth measurement, national percentile and grade-level proficiency reporting, and accessibility supports including read-aloud, text magnification and highlighting. The NCII profile includes grade-specific validity evidence through Grade 11. Its profile is a stronger independent screening reference than relying on vendor claims alone.

**Reports/remediation.** The Student Summary Report shows current scores, trends over time and skill/domain performance; lower-scoring skill areas are routed into a personalized learning path. The wider Exact Path system connects diagnose → instruct → target → analyze and supports student-level and aggregate reporting. The diagnostic can be administered up to five times per academic year.

**Limitations.** The NCII-reviewed profile is specifically the Reading diagnostic; it must not be presented as evidence that speaking, listening, viewing, presenting or extended literary writing are diagnosed. The linked learning-path subscription may be an add-on; current licensing, cost and feature bundle must be verified directly. The publicly listed NCII cost is a US listing and is not a current India quote. Do not assume it is purchasable or commercially suitable for GradiPrep.

**GradiPrep learning:** strong model for domain-level diagnosis, individualized sequencing, accessible supports, repeatable progress checks and a report that explains which skills flow into instruction. Use only as a reading-diagnostic comparator.

Sources:
- [NCII: Exact Path Diagnostic Assessment](https://charts.intensiveintervention.org/screening/tool/?id=9bd6c489782479f8) — independent profile, grade range, evidence, domains, reports, access and accommodations.
- [Edmentum: Understanding Your Student's Diagnostic Results](https://cdn.app.edmentum.com/EdAssets/aa2767aabce44bbf80e372414bffa431) — report and personalized learning path description.
- [Edmentum Exact Path](https://www.edmentum.com/products/exact-path/) — product scope and system overview.

### 3. NWEA MAP Growth Reading + Language Usage — 88/100

**Age/grade fit and constructs.** MAP Growth supports Grades 2+ and has Grade 9 reading and language-usage norms. Reading 6+ reports instructional areas including Literary Text, Informational Text and Vocabulary. Language Usage includes writing/revising for purpose and audience, cohesion/transitions, grammar/usage and mechanics.

**Assessment and evidence.** MAP Growth is computer-adaptive and uses a vertically scaled RIT measure. The 2024–2025 technical report (published April 2026) documents item development, adaptive testing, validity, fairness and score interpretation. It reports Grade 9–12 Language Usage marginal reliability of .97; instructional-area reliability varies with item count and granularity. A 2021 Virginia validation study (ERIC ED618690) found strong technical adequacy and useful reports, while cautioning that the assessment does not cover the full curriculum or highest cognitive demand and should be complemented by other trustworthy indicators. This caveat is particularly important for literary analysis and extended writing.

**Reports/remediation.** Student Profile reports include achievement/growth, instructional-area scores, suggested areas of focus, growth goals and learning statements. This is useful for explaining patterns and monitoring progress.

**Limitations.** MAP Growth is an interim standards-based achievement/growth measure, not a complete diagnostic of MYP Language and Literature. Its item pools and reporting labels depend on selected state/standards alignment. The diagnostic itself does not provide a complete personalized remedial course; teachers must translate the data into instruction. Norms and percentiles are US-referenced and must not be treated as MYP benchmarks or Indian population norms.

**GradiPrep learning:** use the transparent distinction between overall achievement and narrower instructional areas; report measurement error and confidence; avoid trend claims without repeated comparable measures; use domain-level evidence only where enough items support it.

Sources:
- [NWEA MAP Growth Technical Report 2024–2025 (April 2026)](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/MAP-Growth-Technical-Report-2025.pdf).
- [Virginia MAP Growth Validation Study (ERIC ED618690, 2021)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED618690) — external evaluation of item alignment and report usefulness, with explicit limitations.
- [NWEA MAP Growth](https://www.nwea.org/map-growth/) — report types and instructional use.

### 4. Lexia PowerUp Literacy — 83/100

**Age/grade fit and constructs.** PowerUp is designed for Grades 6–12, including Grade 9. It targets Word Study, Grammar and Comprehension, and aims to develop foundational literacy and higher-order analytical skills through adaptive learning paths and teacher-directed offline instruction.

**Assessment and evidence.** Evidence for ESSA lists one cluster-randomized study with 155 Grade 6–8 students, an effect size of +0.36 on STAR Reading after one semester, and a “Promising” evidence rating. A 2021 peer-reviewed cluster-randomized trial also reports significantly higher post-test standardized literacy scores for students using PowerUp than controls. Lexia's 2025 correlational research summary describes relationships between program measures and nine interim/summative reading assessments using data from more than 45,000 students; this is provider-produced correlational evidence, not a randomized causal estimate.

**Reports/remediation.** PowerUp has adaptive learning paths, embedded progress monitoring, actionable data and teacher-directed lessons. Its intervention linkage is one of its strongest features.

**Limitations.** It is primarily an intervention/learning system rather than a broad, standalone diagnostic assessment/report comparable with IXL or MAP. The strongest independent randomized evidence cited by Evidence for ESSA is Grades 6–8, not Grade 9; do not assume identical effect sizes for Year 4. The public evidence reviewed does not establish MYP-specific assessment alignment or full coverage of speaking, listening, viewing and presenting.

**GradiPrep learning:** strong examples of differentiated literacy pathways, explicit foundational teaching, progress monitoring and blending digital practice with teacher-led lessons. It should inform remedial pedagogy more than the overall diagnostic architecture.

Sources:
- [Evidence for ESSA: Lexia PowerUp Literacy](https://www.evidenceforessa.org/program/lexia-powerup-literacy/) — independent evidence rating and study details.
- [Hurwitz & Macaruso (2021), Supporting struggling middle school readers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0193397321000927) — peer-reviewed cluster randomized trial.
- [Lexia PowerUp Literacy Correlational Research (2025)](https://www.lexialearning.com/resources/research/lexia-powerup-literacy-correlational-research).

### 5. GL Assessment Progress Test in English (PTE) — 78/100

**Age/grade fit and constructs.** PTE offers year/level-specific English attainment tests, including reading comprehension, spelling, grammar and punctuation. The independent Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) profile records narrative/non-narrative comprehension, retrieval, simple/complex inference and authorial technique. GL's international-school product information lists ages 5–15, with digital levels 7–15 for international schools.

**Assessment and evidence.** The EEF profile records a UK standardisation sample, validity and reliability information, and reports broken down by English skills and reading comprehension categories. It also notes limitations: the construct evidence is largely face validity and correlation with a previous test version, not an independent validity measure; no criterion-validity information is provided; standardisation dates to 2014. The EEF profile describes the age range as 5–14, while GL's international-school page lists up to 15. For a Grade 9 / MYP Year 4 learner who may be 14 or 15, confirm the correct international level and norm applicability before any use.

**Reports/remediation.** Reports include age-standardised scores, stanines, percentile ranks, individual/group reports and category-level performance. PTE can help identify attainment gaps and support intervention planning.

**Limitations.** PTE is not adaptive; subtests cannot be administered separately; it focuses on technical English and reading comprehension, not the full MYP language-and-literature construct. The EEF review highlights gaps in independent criterion evidence and older standardisation. It does not provide a complete remedial course.

**GradiPrep learning:** useful examples of reporting authorial technique, retrieval and inference separately, as well as grammar/punctuation and narrative/non-narrative reading. Treat the age/norm caveat seriously and do not use UK percentiles as MYP grades.

Sources:
- [Education Endowment Foundation: Progress Test in English](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/measures-database/progress-test-in-english) — independent measure profile, subscales, age range, report outputs and psychometric limitations.
- [GL Assessment: Progress Test in English](https://www.gl-assessment.co.uk/solutions/language-and-literacy/products/progress-test-in-english/) — current product description and international product details.
- [GL international schools PTE information](https://www.gl-education.com/solutions/international-schools/improving-literacy/products/progress-test-in-english/).

## 5. Cross-provider conclusions for GradiPrep

1. **Use a multi-method diagnostic, not a copied commercial test.** Combine objectively scored items with short constructed responses, extended writing, oral/listening evidence and visual/multimodal interpretation where feasible. The MYP's six skill areas cannot be inferred from reading-only or grammar-only results.
2. **Measure literary and non-literary reading separately enough to interpret.** Include retrieval, inference, evidence selection, interpretation, authorial choice, structure, context, audience and purpose, but only after the skill taxonomy is approved.
3. **Writing must be observed, not inferred from grammar scores.** Include sentence control and conventions, but also planning, development, evidence, organisation, cohesion, register and revision through actual written work.
4. **Use response evidence and confidence.** Multiple observations should support a skill judgement. Show insufficient evidence where item coverage is thin or results conflict. Never turn one error into a persistent deficit.
5. **Separate benchmark norms from curriculum mastery.** US state norms, US national norms and UK norms are not MYP grades, IB criteria or Indian cohort norms. Do not show percentiles or grade predictions without a matched and justified reference population.
6. **Build explicit remediation links.** Every identified improvement area must map to a concrete teaching/practice task and a fresh post-course check. Provider recommendations are inspiration, not the final personalised remedial course (five 60-minute sessions recommended; up to seven maximum).
7. **Protect the human review boundary.** The Founder remains the human academic reviewer. Provider research does not authorise automatic grading, a public diagnostic, real learner-data collection or release.

## 6. Evidence gaps and due diligence before any procurement or copying of design patterns

- Obtain current product demos and written confirmation of current Grade 9/age coverage, language variants, accessibility, report exports, item types and data/privacy terms if any product is considered for procurement.
- Verify India availability, pricing, contract terms, data location/retention, parent/student access and school licensing directly. No current India price is inferred in this review.
- Confirm whether each provider's Grade 9 norming/standard-setting population is appropriate for the actual MYP learner population. The reviewed evidence is mainly US or UK; none establishes MYP Year 4 norms.
- Review the full technical documentation and representative public sample reports before relying on any score or category.
- Do not reuse copyrighted test questions, proprietary report text, brand identity, scoring rubrics or restricted teacher materials.
