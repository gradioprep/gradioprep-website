## 2. What this curriculum is — and is not

**Officially described course purpose:** build reading, writing, speaking and listening for different purposes and audiences; help learners respond to varied information, media and texts; develop reading for information and pleasure; and write clearly and creatively for different audiences/purposes. Cambridge recommends a broad mix of fiction, poetry, playscripts and non-fiction.

**Not interchangeable with:** Cambridge Lower Secondary English as a Second Language (0876), generic ESL worksheets, IB MYP Language and Literature, or Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500. IGCSE 0500 is a later qualification with a separate examination syllabus; it is useful as a progression destination, not a reason to teach exam-specific routines too early.

**Assessment context:** Cambridge offers Progression Tests and Cambridge Checkpoint; schools choose their assessment approach. Checkpoint is associated with Stage 9, not a universal Stage 7 high-stakes external exam. Stage 7 should not be reduced to exam practice; use low-stakes checks to inform teaching and next steps.

## 3. Stage 7 learning design: intended endpoint

These are platform learning outcomes informed by public Cambridge curriculum descriptions, not verbatim official objective codes. Verify them against the exact framework before assigning codes or claiming coverage.

By the end of the Stage 7 starter pathway, a learner should increasingly be able to:

1. Read short fiction and non-fiction for meaning, identifying explicit information, plausible inferences and supporting details.
2. Discuss how features of text structure organize information or contribute to an effect, including in poetry where appropriate.
3. Explain a writer's language choices with evidence and relevant vocabulary, moving beyond naming a feature.
4. Plan writing using a suitable method, selecting and organizing ideas for a stated purpose and audience.
5. Compose clear, coherent original texts in more than one form or genre, controlling paragraphing, vocabulary, sentence choices, spelling and punctuation appropriate to the learner's stage.
6. Contribute to discussion by listening, asking or answering relevant questions, explaining an idea and responding constructively to a different view.
7. Use voice, gesture and movement in a short drama/performance task where it serves the text and communication goal.
8. Reflect on a response and identify a precise next step based on task evidence.

These should be converted into individual learning objectives only after comparison with the exact applicable official Stage 7 framework and its progression grid. Do not attach invented Cambridge codes.

## 4. Progression architecture

Use the following as a planning hypothesis across Stages 7–9, then verify it against the complete official progression grid:

- **Stage 7 — notice, select, explain, plan, create:** identify meaningful details and structural features; use a range of planning approaches; explain with textual evidence; communicate a clear response with support.
- **Stage 8 — discuss choices and refine:** explain how text features work for effect; choose a planning approach suited to the task; strengthen interpretation and adaptation to audience/purpose.
- **Stage 9 — analyse, evaluate and choose independently:** analyse structural and language choices for effect; make an informed choice about whether/how to plan; integrate more complex evidence and communication demands as verified by the framework.

These examples paraphrase the progression described by Cambridge's public framework-support overview. They support sequence design but must not replace the official objective codes or complete objective matrix.

### Teaching principles

- Teach reading, writing, speaking and listening in an integrated way where the learning goal benefits from it; avoid four permanently separated silos.
- Teach vocabulary, language, grammar and punctuation in context, with opportunities to recognize and apply concepts.
- Use texts from different times, places and genres, with a deliberate balance of fiction, non-fiction, poetry and playscripts across the stage.
- Include regular opportunities to explore, analyse, collaborate, create, reflect and improve.
- Build independence gradually; longer written answers are not automatically better evidence of every English skill.

### Why this sequence

Unit 1 starts with comprehension and reasoned explanation. Unit 2 transfers attention from reading other people's choices to planning and shaping the learner's own text. Unit 3 gathers direct speaking/listening/performance evidence instead of treating these as decorative add-ons. The sequence supports a broad Stage 7 foundation that can lead into Stage 8; it does not claim to represent Cambridge's official scheme-of-work unit order.

## 6. Unit blueprints

### Unit 1 — Read closely, infer and explain

**Entry check:** Use a brief original text with questions on main idea, explicit detail, a reasonable inference and one language choice. Record confidence separately from task performance.

**Teach/model:** Model how to distinguish explicit information from inference; choose a precise detail; explain why it supports the interpretation. Model a language-choice explanation in context, rather than naming a technique without discussing meaning/effect.

**Guided practice:** Work on one skill at a time: locate detail, infer, select strongest evidence, explain a choice. Include counterexamples where a plausible-sounding inference is not supported by the text.

**Independent transfer:** Use an unseen original text with a comparable but not identical demand. Require a brief explanation that points to the supporting wording/detail.

**Feedback and next step:** If literal meaning is missed, practise locating/combining details; if inference is plausible but unsupported, practise evidence selection; if evidence is accurate but explanation is thin, practise explaining how it supports the idea or how a choice shapes meaning.

**Success evidence:** Relevant understanding, accurate evidence, defensible inference and an explanation connected to the question.

### Unit 2 — Structure, plan and shape a text

**Entry check:** Identify the order of ideas in a short text and produce a quick plan for a familiar prompt.

**Teach/model:** Compare two structures for similar content and discuss what each makes easier for its intended audience. Model a planning choice (list, sequence, mind map, paragraph frame or another suitable method) and show how ideas become a coherent beginning, development and ending.

**Guided practice:** Rearrange paragraphs, improve topic sentences, choose relevant supporting detail, revise a plan for a stated audience/purpose, and apply grammar/spelling/punctuation in context.

**Independent transfer:** Write an original short text in a stated form for a specific audience and purpose, then annotate one structural choice.

**Feedback and next step:** Route learners to ordering/paragraph structure, planning and idea selection, or sentence-level clarity according to response evidence, not a generic low score.

**Success evidence:** A suitable plan, relevant and ordered ideas, clear organization, form appropriate to purpose, and readable language mechanics for the intended task.

### Unit 3 — Communicate, discuss and perform

**Entry check:** Use a short discussion prompt and brief read-aloud/dramatic exchange, with accessible alternatives where appropriate.

**Teach/model:** Model listening for another person's point, paraphrasing before responding, asking a relevant question, and selecting voice/gesture/movement to communicate a character or idea.

**Guided practice:** Practise turn-taking, adding a reason/example, building on or respectfully challenging a view, and rehearsing a short spoken/dramatic segment.

**Independent transfer:** Take part in a structured discussion or present a short interpretation/performance, then reflect on how the chosen mode helped convey meaning.

**Feedback and next step:** Identify one observable strength and one target (relevance, listening/responding, clarity, support for a view or purposeful delivery). Do not infer speaking competence from written performance alone.

**Success evidence:** Relevant contribution, evidence of listening to others, coherent expression and intentional delivery when part of the task.

## 7. Internal formative rubric (platform rubric, not official Cambridge marks)

Score only dimensions relevant to the task on a 0–3 formative scale: 0 = not yet evidenced; 1 = emerging with support; 2 = mostly successful with some gaps; 3 = clear and independent in this task. Record exact evidence and one next action.

| Dimension | Evidence | Next action if weak |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding and inference | Correct reading of explicit information; inference supported by detail | Re-read, annotate and justify a claim with one precise detail |
| Evidence and explanation | Specific support and reasoning linked to the question | Practise claim → evidence → explanation, fading sentence stems over time |
| Structure and planning | Ideas grouped/ordered for the task; plan helps the writing | Use a plan template, then remove scaffolding as choices improve |
| Purpose, audience and creation | Content/form/register fit the intended purpose and audience | Identify the reader and purpose before drafting; evaluate choices against them |
| Speaking/listening/performance | Relevant contribution, listening response and purposeful delivery when assessed | Rehearse one observable subskill and collect direct oral evidence |
| Language control and reflection | Grammar, vocabulary, spelling/punctuation and revision support meaning | Teach the error pattern in context; revise a specific line and explain the change |

This is not Cambridge's official achievement judgement or a proxy for a Progression Test/Checkpoint grade.

## 8. Diagnostic and next-step logic

- Start with a low-stakes sample spanning comprehension, evidence and one short writing action; gather prior-learning context but do not treat one score as a stable ability label.
- Route literal-understanding gaps to close reading/detail selection; unsupported inference to evidence selection; thin explanation to reasoning; disorganized writing to planning/structure; poor audience fit to purpose/register; speaking/listening needs to direct oral practice.
- Use fresh exit tasks to check transfer. Do not declare mastery from completion, clicks or viewing a model answer.
- Provide support, core and stretch variants while preserving the intended skill; stretch through inference quality, evidence selection, control of structure or independence.
- Avoid over-preparing for a single test format in Stage 7. If a school uses Cambridge Progression Tests, align any rehearsal with the relevant stage and current authorised materials; do not reproduce secure test items.

## Part B — Stage 8 chapter

**Pathway ID:** cambridge-ls-english-s8.  
**Status:** Public-framework-aligned design; exact official Stage 8 objective IDs/wording remain blocked pending the applicable framework version.

### Progression intent

Stage 8 should build on Stage 7's evidence selection, supported inference, text structure, planning and purposeful discussion. Students should explain how choices work for effect, select a planning approach more deliberately, and connect their interpretation to audience/purpose. The official progression examples visible in the Cambridge framework-support overview show Stage 8 increasing the demand from commenting on text structure to discussing how a writer uses structure for effect; this is paraphrased as a progression example, not a substitute for the official objective matrix.

### End-of-stage platform outcomes

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- explain how more than one textual choice contributes to an effect;
- select evidence and organize an analytical explanation coherently;
- choose a planning method suited to a writing task rather than rely on one template;
- shape a response for a defined audience and purpose;
- contribute to collaborative discussion, build on another view and explain reasoning;
- develop character, viewpoint or issue through deliberate speech/gesture/movement where drama is appropriate;
- review a draft and make a purposeful improvement.

### Proposed Stage 8 unit sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence | Mapping state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Explain choices and their effects** | Analytical explanation showing how structural/language choices shape a reader's understanding | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |
| 2 | **Choose a plan and control a text** | Original piece with a justified plan/structure and a revision that improves audience fit | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |
| 3 | **Develop viewpoint through discussion and drama** | A reasoned discussion contribution and short performance/reflective response | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |

### Unit blueprints

**Unit 1 — Explain choices and their effects.** Start with a fresh literary or non-literary text. Model how to identify a structural/language choice, locate evidence and explain why the choice matters to the text's meaning or effect. Guided tasks should progress from explanation of one choice to how two choices work together. The independent transfer task uses a different text and expects evidence-led reasoning, not feature-spotting.

**Unit 2 — Choose a plan and control a text.** Compare planning methods and structures for different prompts. Model a purposeful choice, then give learners a prompt for which they select a method, organize ideas and produce an original response. Feedback should focus on whether the plan helped the intended audience follow the idea and whether revision made the text more effective.

**Unit 3 — Develop viewpoint through discussion and drama.** Teach learners to build on, question or respectfully challenge a viewpoint with a reason or example. For a drama/performance task, model how speech, gesture, movement and role shape the audience's understanding. Collect direct spoken/performance evidence and a short reflection; do not substitute written marks for oral skill evidence.

### Stage 8 diagnostic and feedback

Use a low-stakes entry check for analysis of effect, planning choice and discussion. Route learners who only identify features to explanation; those who plan but lose coherence to structure and audience; and those who speak without responding to others to listening and collaborative dialogue. Use a different but comparable exit task to check transfer.

**Readiness for Stage 9 (platform decision):** the learner can explain how choices work for effect, plan more deliberately, sustain a viewpoint and adapt a response with reduced scaffolding. This is not an official Cambridge stage descriptor.

## Part C — Stage 9 chapter

**Pathway ID:** cambridge-ls-english-s9.  
**Status:** Public-framework-aligned design; exact official Stage 9 objective IDs/wording remain blocked pending the applicable framework version.

### Progression intent

Stage 9 consolidates Lower Secondary learning and prepares students for the selected next pathway without pretending that every learner must follow IGCSE First Language English 0500. The public framework-support examples show the demand progressing from discussing text structure (Stage 8) toward analysing how structure can be manipulated for effect (Stage 9), and from choosing an appropriate planning approach toward informed choice about whether/how to plan. These are examples of progression, not a full objective map.

### End-of-stage platform outcomes

Learners should increasingly be able to:
- analyse and evaluate how language, structure and form shape meaning;
- select and organize evidence for a more complex interpretation;
- choose whether and how to plan for a task, then sustain a coherent original text;
- communicate a considered viewpoint in writing and discussion;
- explore a complex issue or character in drama, using delivery choices intentionally;
- evaluate a draft or performance against explicit task-specific criteria and revise;
- identify next-stage learning needs based on evidence rather than an assumed qualification route.

### Proposed Stage 9 unit sequence

| Unit | Proposed focus | Core evidence | Mapping state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Analyse structure, viewpoint and effect** | An evidence-led analysis explaining how choices work together to shape an interpretation | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |
| 2 | **Write independently for purpose and audience** | Original planned or deliberately unplanned response, justified by the task, followed by revision | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |
| 3 | **Explore complex ideas through discussion and drama** | Sustained discussion/performance and reflection connecting evidence, interpretation and delivery choices | Public-framework aligned; exact objective IDs pending |

### Unit blueprints

**Unit 1 — Analyse structure, viewpoint and effect.** Model how a writer can foreground, delay, repeat, contrast or withhold information, then examine a fresh text for the function of its organization. Learners must explain how structure supports an interpretation, not simply label the beginning/middle/end. Use original short texts and questions to avoid reproducing secure Checkpoint material.

**Unit 2 — Write independently for purpose and audience.** Present varied tasks so learners evaluate what planning will help. They may use a full plan, a short outline or another method where suitable; assessment should focus on the quality of the response and the deliberate appropriateness of the approach, not on always filling a planning template. Include audience, purpose, organization, language control and revision.

**Unit 3 — Explore complex ideas through discussion and drama.** Use an original text or issue with more than one defensible perspective. Teach learners to support ideas, respond to counterpoints and use performance choices to shape audience understanding. Evaluate reasoning and listening directly as well as spoken clarity.

### Stage 9 readiness and transition planning

The exit review should identify strengths and gaps in reading comprehension, analysis of choices, writing control, planning, speaking/listening and revision. Recommend a next pathway only from the learner's actual goal, school route and evidence. IGCSE First Language English 0500 may be one possible destination; do not imply every Cambridge Lower Secondary learner must take it or that all Stage 9 content is exam practice.
