# Grade 9 IB MYP Year 4 — Diagnostic Skill Taxonomy and Blueprint

**Version:** 0.1-draft  
**Date:** 31 October 2026  
**Status:** Draft for Founder / human academic review. Not approved for item authoring, implementation, scoring calibration or learner release.  
**Target:** Grade 9 — IB MYP Language and Literature (Founder-confirmed MYP Year 4)  
**Purpose:** Specify the transferable language-and-literature skills to diagnose before curriculum-unit learning, and propose an evidence-balanced assessment blueprint.

## 1. Design principles and official alignment

This is a GradiPrep diagnostic-readiness instrument, not an IB examination, a unit test, an MYP eAssessment replica or an official IB criterion grade.

The public IB MYP Language and Literature framework identifies six skill areas: **listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing and presenting**. It expects engagement with a range of literary and non-literary text types, styles and techniques, including analysis of context, audience, purpose and linguistic/literary devices. The subject is organised through four criteria: **A Analysing, B Organising, C Producing text and D Using language**. The public IB guide describes the four criteria as equally weighted for MYP subject assessment; this does not automatically authorise a GradiPrep diagnostic composite score or official MYP grade conversion.

## 2. Proposed skill taxonomy

### Criterion A — Analysing

| ID | Skill / sub-skill | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Explicit comprehension | Accurately identify central ideas, key details, sequence, claims and relevant information in literary and non-literary texts. |
| A2 | Inference and interpretation | Infer meaning, motivation, viewpoint, implication, tone and ambiguity; distinguish inference from explicit statement. |
| A3 | Context, audience and purpose | Explain how context, audience, purpose, genre and situation shape meaning and interpretation. |
| A4 | Creator's choices | Analyse the effects of diction, imagery, figurative language, syntax, voice, tone, structure, form and visual choices. |
| A5 | Evidence-based reasoning | Select precise textual/visual evidence, explain how it supports an interpretation and use appropriate analytical terminology. |
| A6 | Comparison and connections | Identify and explain meaningful similarities/differences in ideas, perspectives, form, technique and effect across or within texts. |
| A7 | Critical perspective | Recognise bias, representation, reliability, omission and alternative interpretations without assuming one simplistic reading. |

### Criterion B — Organising

| ID | Skill / sub-skill | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Purposeful structure | Choose a form and organisational structure appropriate to task, audience and intention. |
| B2 | Logical sequencing | Develop a clear progression of ideas, events, claims or explanations. |
| B3 | Coherence and cohesion | Use paragraphing, topic focus, transitions, reference and linking devices to make relationships clear. |
| B4 | Development and balance | Sustain a line of thought; select and distribute relevant details/examples rather than listing disconnected points. |
| B5 | Presentation conventions | Apply suitable layout, formatting, signposting and source attribution/reference conventions when the task requires them. |

### Criterion C — Producing text

| ID | Skill / sub-skill | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Idea generation and development | Generate, select and develop relevant ideas with thought, imagination and sensitivity. |
| C2 | Genre and form control | Produce a text that recognises the conventions and possibilities of the chosen literary/non-literary form. |
| C3 | Audience impact | Make deliberate stylistic, linguistic, literary and/or visual choices appropriate to audience, purpose and intended effect. |
| C4 | Perspective and originality | Explore a considered perspective, interpretation or creative direction rather than relying on formulaic reproduction. |
| C5 | Craft and revision | Improve meaning and effect through planning, drafting, editing and purposeful revision. |

### Criterion D — Using language

| ID | Skill / sub-skill | Evidence sought |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Vocabulary | Use precise, varied and context-appropriate vocabulary; interpret vocabulary in context. |
| D2 | Sentence control | Use accurate, varied sentence structures and syntactic choices to communicate meaning. |
| D3 | Grammar and conventions | Apply grammar, punctuation, spelling and usage accurately enough for meaning and purpose. |
| D4 | Register and style | Adapt tone, register and style to audience, purpose, form and context. |
| D5 | Oral language | Communicate ideas intelligibly and fluently with appropriate pronunciation, pace, emphasis and interaction. |
| D6 | Non-verbal communication | Use relevant gesture, posture, expression and visual/oral delivery choices purposefully in a presentation. |

## 3. Six-skill-area coverage crosswalk

| MYP skill area | Primary evidence | Criteria principally informed |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Literary and non-literary text comprehension, inference, interpretation, comparison and evidence selection | A; supporting B and D |
| Viewing | Interpretation of a rights-cleared visual/multimodal text; relationship between visual and verbal choices | A; supporting B |
| Listening | Comprehension, inference, purpose, viewpoint, tone and selection of relevant spoken information | A; supporting D |
| Writing | Analytical and creative/functional written responses | B, C and D; A where analysis is required |
| Speaking | Structured oral response and short follow-up interaction | C and D; supporting A and B |
| Presenting | Purposeful oral/multimodal communication, organisation and audience awareness | B, C and D |

All six skill areas must receive observable evidence. A written multiple-choice test alone cannot be reported as a complete MYP Language and Literature diagnostic.

## 4. Proposed assessment blueprint

**Proposed duration:** approximately 90 minutes, split into two sittings to reduce fatigue. This is a planning estimate, not a locked test duration. A small pilot and Founder review must confirm timing, accessibility and evidence sufficiency before implementation.

| Part | Approx. time | Proposed task / evidence | Main skill and criterion coverage |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Read and view | 22 min | Two short, contrasting stimuli (one literary and one non-literary/multimodal); a balanced set of selected-response and short constructed-response prompts. | Reading, viewing; A1–A7, with B/D evidence where observable |
| 2. Listen | 10 min | One short, clearly recorded spoken stimulus; selected and brief constructed responses on meaning, inference, purpose and tone. | Listening; A1–A4, D5 receptive language support |
| 3. Analytical writing | 23 min | A focused response analysing an unseen text or paired text features, requiring claims, evidence, explanation and coherent structure. | Writing; A, B and D |
| 4. Creative / purposeful production | 20 min | A fresh short text based on an original prompt or rights-cleared visual/text stimulus; learner chooses from a small set of age-appropriate forms. | Writing; B, C and D |
| 5. Speaking / presenting | 15 min | Brief preparation, a 2–3 minute oral response and a short follow-up prompt; assess delivery and audience awareness. | Speaking, presenting; B, C and D |

**Total planned time:** 90 minutes. The table's individual estimates total 90 minutes. Do not silently increase timed task demands to fill the envelope.

### Proposed stimulus and task balance

- Include at least one literary and one non-literary text; choose unfamiliar but age-appropriate contexts and avoid requiring specialist background knowledge.
- Use a visual/multimodal stimulus only after copyright/licence and accessibility checks are completed before selection. A project-original visual is preferred where it meets the task purpose.
- Listening audio must be original or appropriately licensed, intelligible and quality-checked; provide documented access arrangements without unintentionally changing the construct being measured.
- Avoid basing several items on one narrow skill or one stimulus. Each high-impact skill judgement should draw on more than one observable response where feasible.
- Do not reuse existing curriculum-unit passages, questions or model answers as diagnostic items. This is a separate pre-curriculum readiness assessment.
- Keep original passage bodies within the project's existing 250-word rule; longer excerpts must follow the approved public-domain source protocol. Do not assume a visual/audio asset is rights-cleared merely because the text is original.

## 5. Evidence and item coverage proposal

The following is a draft coverage target for blueprint development, not a final item count:

- Reading/viewing: approximately 8–10 scored observations across selected response and short constructed response.
- Listening: approximately 4–5 scored observations, including at least one inference/purpose item.
- Analytical writing: one extended constructed response scored across analysis, organisation and language evidence.
- Creative/purposeful writing: one constructed response scored across production, organisation and language evidence.
- Speaking/presenting: one oral performance plus a brief follow-up, with criterion-linked evidence.
- Embedded language evidence: vocabulary, sentence control, grammar, punctuation, register and conventions observed across more than one task rather than inferred from one isolated grammar quiz.

Every item/task must map to one or more taxonomy IDs. The item map must identify which sub-skills receive direct evidence, indirect evidence or no evidence. No sub-skill may be declared secure solely because a learner answered one selected-response item correctly.

## 6. Scoring, confidence and reporting — proposed controls

1. Use criterion-linked analytic rubrics for constructed and oral responses; use item-level scoring for selected responses. Document accepted alternatives and partial-credit rules.
2. Report the four criterion-aligned profiles separately and with equal visual prominence. Do not create an overall MYP grade, convert diagnostic results to the IB 1–7 scale, or claim official IB moderation.
3. Do not assume that the four criteria's equal weighting in MYP subject assessment requires a single equally weighted GradiPrep diagnostic total. Any composite score or weighting needs a separate validity rationale and Founder approval.
4. Attach evidence IDs, task types and confidence to each sub-skill judgement. Distinguish direct observation from inferred evidence.
5. Use an explicit insufficient-evidence state. Conflicting evidence should trigger a cautious interpretation or a further probe, not an arbitrary average.
6. Do not show provider percentiles or rankings in learner-facing reports. Do not show learner performance percentiles, grade predictions or population comparisons without a current, appropriately matched and documented norming basis.
7. Student-facing descriptions must be constructive, strengths-based and actionable. Avoid permanent labels or deficit language.

**Scoring scale and thresholds:** Not set in this draft. The Founder must approve the rubric language, evidence sufficiency rules, aggregation and priority thresholds after reviewing the taxonomy and blueprint. No IB 1–8 criterion levels or 1–7 subject-grade conversion is authorised.

## 7. Remediation linkage

Design for **five 60-minute sessions wherever the analytics show that five sessions can comprehensively address the needs**. Extend to six, then seven only where the evidence-based coverage plan shows that five (or six) would leave a material improvement area insufficiently taught or practised. Seven sessions is an absolute maximum. Do not add sessions merely to make the course appear more extensive.

The future teacher/editor pack must include, for each improvement area: diagnostic evidence IDs, confidence, priority rationale, target taxonomy IDs, planned session/task IDs, teaching model, guided practice, independent transfer, success evidence and post-course check mapping.

## 8. Feasibility and exclusions

- Current product architecture is React/TypeScript with no authentication, database or persistent learner records. The first prototype must use synthetic responses and local, non-persistent state unless the Founder separately approves additional infrastructure.
- No real student data, voice recording upload, cross-session saved reports or longitudinal progress may be claimed or implemented without an approved privacy and architecture design.
- Speaking may be teacher-observed or assessed through a locally completed oral task in the prototype; do not imply a recording is saved or transmitted.
- Audio, visual and accessibility support must be designed without paid services, external APIs or new infrastructure unless separately approved.
- Existing design system, teacher-pack separation, content rights, accessibility, academic review and release gates remain binding.
