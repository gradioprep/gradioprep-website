## 2. Framework baseline and currency control

### 2.1 Current publicly documented MYP context

Use the public framework as the non-negotiable design floor:
- build learning across all six Language and Literature skill areas: listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing and presenting;
- engage learners with a broad mix of literary and non-literary forms;
- attend to context, audience, purpose, text choices and linguistic/literary devices;
- make inquiry, conceptual understanding, reflection and learner agency visible;
- develop skills for analysis and for creating their own texts, not analysis alone;
- keep assessment evidence and learner feedback tied to intended learning rather than treating a worksheet score as mastery.

The IB public page does not prescribe our six unit titles, their order, a standard MYP Year 1 textbook, or one universal external Year 1 exam. Schools design teaching and school-based assessment in their context, using applicable prescribed objectives and criteria.

### 2.2 Legacy objective crosswalk — reference only

The indexed official detailed guide for use from 2014/15 contains four criteria at Year 1:
- **A — Analysing:** noticing significant aspects of texts and creator choices; using evidence and explanation to support ideas; identifying similarities/differences where appropriate.
- **B — Organizing:** organizing ideas for context and intention; communicating a logical through-line; using referencing/formatting appropriate to the intended presentation.
- **C — Producing text:** developing thoughtful/imaginative responses and perspectives; making language/literary/visual choices with some awareness of audience; selecting relevant details/examples to support meaning.
- **D — Using language:** building vocabulary and sentence control; using an appropriate register/style; applying grammar, syntax, punctuation, spelling and/or pronunciation; using non-verbal communication where relevant.

This is a concise paraphrase of indexed legacy Year 1 criterion descriptions, not a claim that the 2014/15 wording is the latest valid edition. A current authenticated guide, official date and any revised descriptors must be obtained/verified before this crosswalk is promoted to a current official mapping. The internal curriculum may use these criteria as a **legacy audit lens**; the learner-facing product metadata must leave official objective/criterion mappings unverified until that check has passed.

### 2.3 Enhanced MYP transition watch

The IB's beta guidance identifies 2027 as the planned full-release year for Enhanced MYP. Specialist updates discuss possible changes to unit-planning language and subject-specific guidance; do not treat these as finalized IB requirements.

Maintain separate fields for:
- current/legacy framework reference and verification status;
- Enhanced MYP beta information and source date;
- final publication/effective date and revised subject guide when available;
- impact assessment and approval status for any mapping changes.

Do **not** mix beta terminology with current framework terms in a single “official requirements” section. If the final guide changes unit planner fields, objectives or criteria, version and remap the affected units rather than silently rewriting previous decisions.

- **Vertical progression:** look for concepts or skills that return across years at greater depth, especially narrative, audience, perspective, form and language choices.
- **Lesson architecture:** combine explicit modelling, vocabulary support, guided practice, independent transfer, formative feedback and reflection.
- **Implementation:** attach every unit to outcomes, learning experiences, task evidence, source/version metadata, review states and a next-step rule.
- **Quality assurance:** label each resource as official, legacy official, independent specialist, publisher, platform workflow or unverified marketplace item.

## 4. Year 1 pathway outcomes

**Audience:** beginning MYP Language and Literature learners. Age, school grade, English proficiency and prior curriculum exposure may vary, so entry checks must guide support rather than label students.

By the end of this starter pathway, a learner should increasingly be able to:
1. Understand and respond to short literary and non-literary texts in print, audio, visual and multimodal forms.
2. identify a central idea, perspective, purpose and significant detail, with appropriate support;
3. choose relevant evidence and explain how it supports an interpretation;
4. describe how selected linguistic, structural, literary or visual choices can shape meaning and audience response;
5. identify how voice, point of view and context affect representation;
6. organize a response for a stated purpose, form and audience;
7. create, revise and present short original texts using deliberate choices;
8. listen to others, build on an idea and communicate a reasoned viewpoint;
9. reflect on specific evidence of success and choose a justified next step.

These are **platform outcomes** derived from the public subject framework and the legacy-guidance comparison, not official quotations or a claim that the official Year 1 guide has been fully mapped.

## 5. Proposed six-unit Year 1 scope and sequence

The sequence is a platform design decision. It is deliberately broader than the initial three-unit sketch and aims to distribute the six skills across a full year. Final text selection must be age-appropriate, culturally responsive, accessible and rights-cleared.

| Unit | Working title | Core inquiry / learning focus | Skill blend | Key learner evidence | Legacy-reference lens* | Planned hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | **How writers and creators shape meaning** | How can we move from noticing a choice to explaining an effect? Close reading/viewing, claim–evidence–explanation, audience response. | Reading, viewing, writing, speaking/listening | Annotated short text/visual; evidence-led explanation; fresh-text transfer | A + B + D; C when the learner creates a response | 7 |
| 2 | **Stories that travel: myths, legends and adaptation** | How do stories preserve, question or change beliefs across contexts? Narrative patterns, retelling, oral tradition and adaptation. | Listening, speaking, reading, viewing, writing | Compare a short story and retelling; brief adaptation with rationale | A + C + D; B for sequencing and format | 7 |
| 3 | **Who gets to tell the story? Voice, identity and perspective** | How do narration, character, viewpoint and detail influence who is heard? | Reading, listening, writing, speaking | Supported interpretation of perspective; short original narrative or monologue; revision note | A + C + D; B for coherent shaping | 7 |
| 4 | **Do advertisements run the world? Persuasion and media literacy** | How do creators target audiences and invite belief or action? Purpose, rhetoric, evidence, tone, image/text relations and credibility. | Viewing, reading, speaking, writing, listening | Analyse a short original/cleared campaign; produce a persuasive message; justify choices | A + B + C + D | 7 |
| 5 | **Poetry and image: language, sound and response** | How do sound, imagery, structure and visual relationships shape mood or interpretation? | Reading, viewing, listening, speaking/presenting, writing | Annotated poem/visual pair; short poem or response; read-aloud or multimodal presentation | A + C + D; B for sequence/form | 7 |

Speech structure, claim/evidence, rhetorical choices, listening and response. | Listening, speaking, reading, writing, presenting | Plan and deliver/write a short speech; identify evidence and opposing view; reflect on audience effect | A + B + C + D | 7 |

*Legacy-reference lens is a planning/audit aid based on the indexed 2014/15 detailed guide, not a verified current official mapping. The six units do not represent a required IB sequence. School calendars may allocate more time; do not shrink or relabel the annual minimum without checking the relevant programme rules.*

### Why this sequence

1. Unit 1 establishes the reusable claim–evidence–explanation habit and may be reconciled with the existing preview after approval.
2. Units 2–3 move from narrative patterns to perspective and identity, extending interpretation into purposeful text creation.
3. Unit 4 deliberately widens beyond literature and includes media literacy and audience/purpose.
4. Unit 5 combines close reading/viewing with oral, written and multimodal response.
5. Unit 6 integrates argument, evidence, listening, speaking and presentation.
6. Shared hours allow diagnosis, language support, retrieval, revision and transfer across genres instead of treating unit boundaries as skill boundaries.

## 6. Learning and assessment architecture

Every unit should use the following adaptable cycle. This is the platform's instructional design, not an IB-mandated sequence.

1. **Inquiry and entry check:** introduce a meaningful question; obtain a low-stakes first response.
2. **Explicit goal and success evidence:** state the skill in accessible language and show what evidence would demonstrate it.
3. **Model:** think aloud through one challenging decision; annotate a short original or rights-cleared model.
4. **Guided practice:** rehearse one decision at a time with prompts and corrective feedback.
5. **Deliberate practice:** vary the texts and reduce prompts progressively; include at least one misconception or weak example to diagnose.
6. **Independent transfer:** apply the skill to a fresh text or task.
7. **Feedback and revision:** identify one evidence-based strength, one priority improvement and an opportunity to revise.
8. **Reflection and next step:** use task evidence to choose a small, specific follow-up rather than rely on learner self-rating alone.

### Task and rubric rules

- Use a mix of formative and summative evidence; avoid over-relying on long written answers.
- Gather direct evidence for speaking, listening, viewing and presenting when these are learning goals.
- Internal 0–3 labels may help concise formative feedback, but must be called **platform rubric levels**, not IB achievement levels.
- When legacy A–D lenses are used, document them as a historical planning crosswalk and do not claim current official alignment.
- Do not assign official objective/criterion codes in the app until the correct current guide edition has been validated.
- Make feedback actionable: quote the learner's own evidence where suitable, identify the move that worked/did not work, provide a revision action, and re-check on a fresh example.

## 7. Unit-level design requirements

Each unit's detailed plan must contain:
1. unit purpose and central inquiry question;
2. target learners, prerequisites and common misconceptions;
3. source/version confidence and status label for all curriculum statements;
4. relevant framework concepts/contexts/ATL only where their current status is verified;
5. measurable learner outcomes and success evidence;
6. a range of original/cleared texts with genre, mode, culture/context, estimated reading demand and rights record;
7. vocabulary and language-form support as needed;
8. annotated exemplar and teacher think-aloud;
9. guided, independent and transfer tasks of increasing independence;
10. task-specific feedback rules and a fair internal rubric;
11. direct opportunities for intended listening/speaking/viewing/presenting outcomes;
12. differentiation through scaffolds, access and stretch without removing the target skill;
13. accessibility, cultural/sensitive-content and rights checks;
14. teacher/editor answer notes kept separate from learner-facing materials;
15. source trail, version, reviewer, approval state and recheck date.

Not every unit requires every task format; the exact evidence must fit its learning goal.

## 8. Inclusion, text selection and rights

- Prefer original or demonstrably rights-cleared passages, images and audio.
- Maintain a rights ledger with author/creator, title, source, licence/permission basis, adaptation details and permitted distribution.
- Do not copy InThinking, ASJ, Hachette, ManageBac, Toddle, marketplace or examination content into the product merely because a description or sample is public.
- Ensure broad perspectives without using a single text as a proxy for a culture or group.
- Check vocabulary load, reading length, visual load, cultural assumptions, sensitive material and accessibility.
- Use support/core/stretch paths to vary modelling, prompts and complexity while keeping the intended skill explicit.
- Include reading, listening, speaking, writing, viewing and presenting over the year; do not silently assess a skill through a different mode.
- The proposed six units are not a substitute for each learner's school curriculum or for a complete five-year MYP programme.

## 9. Quality and readiness review

Before a unit is treated as draft-complete, reviewers must verify:
- curriculum claims against the correct source and edition;
- teaching sequence and task-to-outcome coherence;
- age/reading suitability and cultural/context sensitivity;
- feedback fairness, answer keys and rubric clarity;
- rights to use all text, media, screenshots and model responses;
- accessibility and assistive-technology behaviour;
- separation between learner materials and teacher/editor keys;
- direct task evidence for each skill claimed;
- next-step logic tested against sample learner responses;
- accurate pathway status in the user interface.

Academic review, official mapping verification, rights review, accessibility review and release approval are distinct gates. A pass on one cannot substitute for the others.
