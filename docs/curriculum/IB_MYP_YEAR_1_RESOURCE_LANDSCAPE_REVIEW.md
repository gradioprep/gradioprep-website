# IB MYP Language and Literature Year 1 — EdTech and Resource Landscape Review

**Version:** 1.0  
**Research checked:** 26 October 2026  
**Status:** Completed desk-research audit for founder review; this is a research record, not an IB endorsement or permission to reproduce third-party materials.  
**Purpose:** Identify MYP-specific teaching platforms, publisher resources and implementation tools that can inform an independently designed Year 1 pathway without confusing third-party practice material with official requirements.

## 1. Executive judgement

The first Year 1 guide used too narrow a resource search. It depended mainly on the IB's public subject overview and general teaching-evidence syntheses. That was not sufficient for a curriculum-director-level scan of the specialist EdTech and teaching-resource landscape.

The revised research separates four different evidence jobs:

1. **Official framework and assessment facts:** use the IB's public pages and any detailed IB document only with an explicit edition/version label.
2. **MYP-specific unit and task design:** study specialist teaching platforms, publisher samples and educator resources as comparative examples; verify curriculum claims separately.
3. **Implementation architecture:** examine unit-planning, assessment, learning-management and review workflows from teacher platforms.
4. **Pedagogy and evaluation:** use independent evidence syntheses for modelling, deliberate practice, feedback, reflection, inclusion and transfer. These inform instructional choices but do not define IB requirements.

**Key finding:** the public subject page is current, but the publicly indexed detailed MYP Language and Literature guide located during this review identifies itself as for use from September 2014 or January 2015. It gives useful historical Year 1 criterion descriptors, but does not establish the latest current edition. The Enhanced MYP is in beta, with the IB stating a full release is scheduled for 2027. Therefore the curriculum must maintain a versioned baseline and a separate transition watchlist rather than blend older and forthcoming terms.

## 2. Source evaluation rules

Each source is labelled by its actual role, not its marketing claims. A third-party site can be excellent for a teaching pattern and still be weak evidence for an official objective. A publisher's unit sequence is an example, not the required IB sequence. A practice eAssessment task is not an official specimen paper. A school-management platform is evidence about workflow design, not proof that its content is correct.

Access and copyright controls apply throughout:
- No bypassing subscriptions, school portals or paywalls.
- Public title pages, descriptions, excerpts and samples may be reviewed; protected books, lessons, model responses and assessment materials must not be copied into the product without the proper licence.
- Record the date, edition, course/year and sample type before using any assessment-related claim.
- Create original learner-facing passages and tasks, unless a specific licensed/permissioned use is documented.
- Publicly indexed snippets are discovery evidence; verify the linked source and context before making high-stakes curriculum claims.

## 4. Cross-source design insights

Across the useful MYP specialist/publisher resources, the recurring pattern is not a single prescribed topic list. It is a sequence that connects a meaningful inquiry or context to explicit language/text learning, gives the learner models and vocabulary, includes varied practice, and makes formative and summative evidence visible. We adopt that pattern as a design principle while writing original material.

The revised Year 1 pathway must therefore:
- cover listening, speaking, reading, writing, viewing and presenting across the whole year, with direct evidence for each intended skill;
- include literary and non-literary forms, print and visual/multimodal communication, different audiences/purposes and culturally broad text choices;
- teach both interpretation and production; avoid six units of isolated reading-analysis worksheets;
- balance explicit teaching, modelling, guided practice, independent transfer, revision and reflection;
- show the alignment trail from source/version → learning outcome → task → evidence → feedback → next step;
- leave room for school/local-context choices rather than implying these six proposed units are an IB-mandated sequence.

## 5. Current framework and Enhanced MYP transition

The IB's current public pages describe the current MYP frame in terms of key and related concepts, global contexts, approaches to learning (ATL), inquiry/action/reflection, subject-specific aims/objectives and assessment. The IB's indexed 2014/15 Language and Literature guide includes Years 1/3/5 criterion descriptors for A Analysing, B Organizing, C Producing text and D Using language.

The IB's beta unit-planning guidance says the Enhanced MYP is scheduled for full release in 2027; the research describes the beta as focused on unit planning. Specialist InThinking updates discuss anticipated changes, but those updates are not official final guidance. Therefore:
- keep the existing/currently documented MYP planning fields distinct from future beta terminology;
- maintain a transition watchlist, not a parallel purported syllabus;
- when the final Enhanced MYP digital guide is released, record official edition, effective date, scope of changes and exact subject guide before remapping;
- do not replace current framework labels, objective codes or rubrics with beta labels simply because they appear in a third-party platform.

## 6. Resulting recommendation for Year 1

Replace the initial three-unit outline with a six-unit annual pathway plus shared diagnostic/transfer sessions, for a planned total of at least 50 guided teaching hours. This is an independent product design decision, not an IB-prescribed sequence.

Proposed sequence:
1. How writers and creators shape meaning (keeps the existing preview as a candidate; do not duplicate it).
2. Stories that travel: myths, legends and adaptation.
3. Who gets to tell the story? Voice, identity and perspective.
4. Do advertisements run the world? Persuasion, audience and media literacy.
5. Poetry and image: language, sound, visual response and performance.
6. Speaking up: speeches, evidence, counter-perspectives and purposeful presentation.

The sequence intentionally spirals analysis into interpretation, then production and performance. Final topics, texts and hours remain subject to founder review, local student suitability, source/rights checks, accessibility and future official-guide crosswalk.

## 7. What remains unresolved

- The exact current detailed MYP subject-guide edition and whether the 2014/15 Year 1 descriptor wording still applies unchanged.
- The final Enhanced MYP published guide and effective implementation requirements.
- Access to gated official sample assessments and school-only teacher-support material.
- Independent learner/teacher usability testing and local India-based needs research.
- Rights clearance for every specific text, image, excerpt and model.
- Whether every proposed task maps sufficiently to current Year 1 criteria once the correct guide is verified.

This review improves the research base; it does not certify official curriculum mapping, release readiness or IB endorsement.
