## 1. Purpose and boundaries

This document converts the approved taxonomy and high-level blueprint into a task-level evidence plan. It identifies the evidence each task must collect, the direct and supporting subskills, response modes, timing, scoring approach and evidence-confidence controls.

This is GradiPrep's Preliminary Assessment (PA), not an IB examination, official MYP assessment, eAssessment replica or official IB criterion grade. The PA samples only question types and skills directly mapped to MYP Language and Literature criteria/strands. The six broad MYP skill areas are not a requirement for six standalone PA sections. The four MYP criteria inform the evidence map; they do not authorise an IB grade conversion, a single composite score or a claim of official IB moderation.

Question and stimulus wording remains subject to exact-version academic review. The PA excludes Listening L01–L05. A separate Listening task is reserved for M3/SA3, never PA, SA1 or SA2. Correct answers, distractor rationales, ideal responses and private scoring anchors belong in a genuinely private, access-controlled teacher system, not this public GitHub repository. This document does not set final numerical cut scores, weighting, learner priority thresholds or automated grading logic.

## 3. Task-level item map

### 3.1 Read and view — 25 minutes

Use two contrasting stimuli: (a) an age-appropriate literary text and (b) a non-literary text with a meaningful visual/multimodal component. The stimuli must not depend on specialist background knowledge. Prefer project-original material where suitable. An original passage body may be no longer than 250 words under the current project rule; any longer passage must follow the approved recognised-writer public-domain protocol. Select visual assets only after rights/licence and accessibility checks have been completed. Do not reuse existing curriculum-unit passages, questions or model answers.

| ID | Intended evidence / response | Direct subskills | Supporting evidence | Draft scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RV01 | Identify central idea, relevant explicit details and accurate information from the literary and/or non-literary stimulus; include more than one observation. | A1 | A5 where evidence selection is required | Keyed response for closed parts; partial credit only where defined in advance |
| RV02 | Interpret an implied meaning, viewpoint, motivation or implication and support the inference with a relevant detail. | A2, A5 | A1 | Short constructed response with an evidence-and-inference rubric |
| RV03 | Explain the effect of a deliberate creator choice (for example diction, imagery, syntax, voice, tone or structure) in its specific context. | A4, A5 | A2 | Short constructed response; accept defensible interpretations supported by the stimulus |
| RV04 | Identify audience/purpose and explain how a specific poster wording or layout choice supports that purpose in context. | A3, A4 | A1 | Selected audience/purpose plus contextual choice-and-effect explanation; selection alone is insufficient |
| RV05 | Interpret a specific visual/outcome feature and explain how the poster's organisation and figure together communicate meaning. | A1, A2, A4 | A5 | Selected figure plus brief graphic-and-verbal explanation; selection alone cannot evidence A.ii |
| RV06 | Analyse how a text's wording and presented evidence shape a perspective or interpretation, including its limits; do not assess external-source credibility or generic media reliability. | A7 | A2, A5 | Short constructed response with evidence and qualification |
| RV07a | Compare the perspectives or central ideas in two texts; explain one meaningful similarity or difference using relevant evidence. | A6, A5 | A2 | Short comparative response; reward explained comparison, not a list of differences |
| RV07b | Compare how the two texts use form, structure or creator choices to shape a related idea or effect. | A6, A4 | A5 | Short comparative response; distinguish comparison of technique/effect from feature-spotting |
| RV08 | Track sequence, progression or structural development and explain how the order/arrangement affects meaning. | A1, A4 | B2 when the learner's explanation is organised | Selected ordering or short constructed response, chosen to suit the stimulus |
| RV09 | Select the most relevant evidence for a stated interpretation/claim and explain why it supports that claim better than a weaker or unrelated detail. | A5 | A1, A2 | Keyed evidence selection plus brief justification; distractor rationales required |

Do not let one stimulus dominate all nine observations. RV01–RV03 should not all depend on the same small passage detail; RV04–RV06 should be anchored in the non-literary/multimodal stimulus; RV07 should use a genuinely comparable pair or two well-defined features. RV08 may use either stimulus if the structural evidence is clear.

### 3.2 Language in context — 10 minutes

Use brief, meaningful sentences or micro-contexts drawn from original diagnostic material. Do not turn this section into a decontextualised grammar drill. Each task must test a decision that affects meaning, clarity or suitability.

| ID | Intended evidence / response | Direct subskills | Supporting evidence | Draft scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LC01 | Select a contextual meaning and explain which specific words in the microcontext support that interpretation; a selected answer alone is insufficient evidence for A.i. | D1 | A1 | Keyed response plus brief rationale where needed |
| LC02 | Explain how the rearranged time phrase changes sentence emphasis and what the reader notices first, while preserving chronological meaning. | D2, A4 | A1 | Keyed selection plus explanatory comparison; do not infer A.ii from selection alone |
| LC03 | Identify or correct a grammar/usage issue while preserving the intended meaning. | D3 | D2 | Keyed edit; record acceptable variants |
| LC04 | Use punctuation and sentence boundaries to make relationships and meaning clear. | D3 | D2 | Keyed edit or selection; do not score a stylistic preference as an error |
| LC05 | Adapt a short message to a specified audience, purpose and context, maintaining an appropriate register and tone. | D4 | D1, D2, D3 | Brief rewrite scored against task-specific register and meaning anchors |

### 3.3 Analytical writing — 20 minutes

Use one unseen, focused analytical prompt linked to a diagnostic stimulus, not to an existing curriculum unit. The proposed response guidance is **150–200 words, ideal target 180 words**, subject to pilot timing and academic review. The word target is guidance, not a maximum; do not truncate, reject or block an over-target response. Provide the visible current/ideal word counter if the task is implemented in the interface.

| ID | Scoring dimension | Direct subskills | Evidence expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| AW01 | Interpretive claim and line of reasoning | A2 | A clear, relevant interpretation that goes beyond retelling and is developed consistently |
| AW02 | Analysis of creator choices and effects | A4 | Explanation of how a specific choice contributes to meaning/effect in context |
| AW03 | Evidence selection and explanation | A5 | Relevant, accurate textual/visual evidence with an explicit reasoning link to the claim |
| AW04 | Organisation and development | B1, B2, B3, B4 | A purposeful structure, logical progression, coherent links and balanced development rather than disconnected points |
| AW05 | Language control and register | D1, D2, D3, D4 | Precise vocabulary, controlled sentences, accurate conventions and an appropriate analytical register |

A strong response is not defined by length alone, and a learner should not be rewarded for padding. Score what the response demonstrates in each dimension. A grammatical slip should not erase a strong interpretation; do not double-penalise one error across unrelated dimensions.

### 3.4 Creative / purposeful production — 15 minutes

Use one original prompt with a clearly stated audience and purpose and one controlled form/genre. A short narrative scene is a workable pilot candidate; the final genre and prompt remain to be reviewed. The proposed response guidance is **100–150 words, ideal target 125 words**, subject to pilot timing and academic review. Allow brief planning and a visible edit/revision step. Word count is guidance only and must not restrict entry.

| ID | Scoring dimension | Direct subskills | Evidence expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| CW01 | Development of ideas/perspectives in the produced text | C1 | The final scene visibly develops a purposeful idea or perspective; do not infer an unseen idea-generation process |
| CW02 | Purposeful structure and stylistic form choices | B1, C2 | The final scene visibly organises its narrative for its intended context and uses deliberate stylistic choices; genre labelling alone is insufficient |
| CW03 | Audience impact | C3 | Language and stylistic choices are deliberately suited to the stated reader and intended effect |
| CW04 | Purposeful perspective and imaginative development | C1 | A perspective is imaginatively developed and evidenced in the final text; novelty is not separately scored |
| CW05 | Observable revision trace and final organisation | B2, C1 (conditional) | Retain the initial wording, revised wording and explanation; assess organisation in the final text under B.ii and claim C.i process evidence only when the trace actually shows development |

The CW05 trace is essential: without an observable draft-to-revision change or documented revision decision, the task cannot support a confident judgement about C5. Do not infer revision skill from the polished final text alone.

### 3.5 Speaking / presenting — 10 minutes

The task should assess purposeful oral communication without requiring recording, upload, paid services or an external API. Use a standardised prompt and a simple outline/presentation card prepared during the task. The card contains a title, a short sequence of points and an appropriate visual/signposting cue; it is not a slide-design exercise.

| ID | Scoring dimension | Direct subskills | Evidence expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| SP01 | Structure and presentation conventions | B1, B5, C2 | A purposeful outline/card with a clear form, title, logical signposting and a layout that supports the message |
| SP02 | Sequence, coherence and development | B2, B3, B4 | Spoken ideas progress logically, connect clearly and include enough relevant explanation/example |
| SP03 | Audience and intended effect | C3 | Delivery and choices are suited to the stated audience and purpose |
| SP04 | Considered viewpoint | C4 | The learner communicates a relevant perspective or interpretation rather than reciting unrelated points |
| SP05 | Oral register, pronunciation and non-verbal communication | D2, D5, D6 | Record separate observed evidence of spoken register/style, intelligible pronunciation and meaningful non-verbal choices; do not score interaction as an independent strand or reward accent conformity, extroversion or normative eye contact |

**Proposed 10-minute sequence:** 2 minutes to prepare the card, up to 3 minutes to present, up to 2 minutes for a standardised follow-up, and 3 minutes for transition/rater notes. The assessor records observations locally in the teacher/editor scoring sheet; no voice recording is assumed or retained. Do not score accent conformity, extroversion, eye contact as a cultural norm or disability-related speech characteristics as a proxy for literary/language competence. Assess intelligibility and communicative effectiveness in context.

## 6. Scoring specification — draft, not calibrated

### 6.1 Response-level scoring

- **Selected-response items:** score against a pre-validated key. Use full credit for the correct answer and no credit for an incorrect answer unless the item has an explicitly documented partial-credit structure. Do not infer a misconception from a distractor alone.
- **Short constructed responses:** use task-specific analytic anchors. Award credit for valid, source-supported reasoning, not exact model wording. Document acceptable alternatives and the evidence that distinguishes a supported inference from an unsupported guess.
- **Extended writing and oral performance:** score each listed dimension separately. Do not allow fluency, length, spelling or confidence to inflate an unrelated analysis/production dimension. Record evidence excerpts/observations for each judgement.
- **Language-in-context edits:** distinguish an objectively incorrect form from a defensible stylistic choice. Preserve meaning and accepted variants in the key.

### 6.2 Provisional descriptive bands

For pilot scoring discussion only, consider four developmental descriptions for each analytic dimension: **Emerging**, **Developing**, **Secure in this task**, and **Strong / transferable evidence**. Add a separate **Insufficient evidence** state. These labels are a proposed working vocabulary, not IB criterion levels, not a validated numeric scale and not approved learner-facing report wording.

Do not assign numerical thresholds, percentages, criterion weights, a combined score, a grade prediction or an IB 1–7 conversion at this stage. The Founder must approve the rubric language and aggregation approach after reviewing the complete scoring pack and pilot evidence. Criterion A, B, C and D profiles must have equal visual prominence in any future report; this visual decision does not imply a single equally weighted diagnostic total.

### 6.3 Evidence confidence and sufficiency

Record each observation as **direct**, **supporting/indirect**, or **not observed**. For each subskill retain the evidence IDs, response mode, scorer note and any contradiction.

Proposed conservative rules for pilot validation:

1. One correct closed-response item is not enough to label a subskill secure.
2. A confident strength normally requires at least two independent, consistent direct observations; use more than one response mode where the skill permits.
3. A material improvement priority normally requires converging evidence from at least two observations, or one consequential performance observation corroborated by another relevant source. A single isolated slip is a probe flag, not a confirmed deficit.
4. Mark **Insufficient evidence** when the task was not completed, an access barrier invalidated the observation, only indirect evidence exists, the evidence conflicts materially, or the response does not permit the intended skill to be judged.
5. Keep low-confidence, moderate-confidence and higher-confidence judgements distinct in the teacher/editor record. Exact evidence-count rules and report thresholds remain subject to pilot validation and Founder approval.

Do not average contradictory evidence into false precision. Where a judgement is uncertain, record the conflict, use cautious report wording and recommend a targeted follow-up probe rather than forcing a category.

### 6.4 Remediation routing

Do not yet set numerical Priority 5–1 thresholds. Once the rubric and evidence sufficiency rules are validated, map each confirmed improvement area to:
- evidence IDs and confidence;
- the relevant taxonomy ID(s);
- the reason the skill matters for the learner's next learning;
- one or more specific remedial teaching/practice tasks;
- success evidence and a fresh post-course check item/construct.

Prefer five 60-minute sessions where they can comprehensively address all evidenced needs. Extend to six or seven only when the coverage analysis justifies it; seven is the strict maximum. Do not author remedial lessons during this item-map stage.

## 8. Accessibility, fairness and rights controls

- Check passage and visual rights/licences before selecting or using PA stimuli. Record source, author/creator, licence/territory, date checked and permitted uses. No separate Listening audio stimulus is part of this PA.
- Keep original passage bodies within 250 words unless the approved public-domain excerpt process is followed for a longer work. The text-length allowance does not authorise third-party visual/audio use.
- Provide clear instructions, readable layout, keyboard-operable response modes and accessible equivalents where they do not invalidate the construct.
- Predefine accommodations and document whether each changes the construct being assessed. Never interpret an inaccessible task as a learner skill deficit.
- Check text readability, visual contrast, alt text and meaningful non-colour cues. For live Speaking/Presenting, review fair access and communication accommodations without turning accent conformity or speech recording into an extra tested skill.
- Avoid culturally narrow assumptions, unnecessary idioms, specialist background knowledge and prompts that advantage one social or language background without relevance to the target construct.
- For oral performance, judge communication rather than personality, extroversion, accent conformity or culturally specific presentation habits.

## 9. Pilot validation plan

Before fixing scoring rules or implementing:

1. Have the Founder / human academic reviewer inspect every task specification, skill tag, evidence rule and proposed response demand.
2. Author a small representative set of items only after this map is approved, then review item clarity, ambiguity, task demand, timing and stimulus independence.
3. Pilot timing and accessibility with appropriate volunteers or a controlled, consented process; do not use real learner data in public previews or unapproved systems.
4. Double-score a defined subset of constructed and oral responses during the pilot; record disagreements, revise anchors and document the final calibration method.
5. Check item difficulty, distractor performance, omissions, completion time, response burden and possible stimulus dependence. Do not claim psychometric validity from a small pilot.
