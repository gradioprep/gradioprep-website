## 1. Intended learning evidence

Learners should be able to locate a direct clue, explain a character's inference using evidence, reconstruct the main event sequence, use common location prepositions in context, and retell a short narrative coherently.

These are internal learning goals informed by a public source-context check; they are not copied official CBSE outcome wording.

## 2. Suggested answers and rationale

### Task 1 — Direct clue

**Expected choice:** B — the red cloth strip leads towards the fallen log.  
**Rationale:** Hoofprints are a clue but do not prove that the deer moved the marker. The cloth trail and the marker's position provide the relevant evidence for locating it.

### Task 2 — Inference

**Strong response characteristics:** Piku recognises that he guessed the deer was responsible before he had checked the evidence. He apologises after finding that the marker had been moved by wind and caught on a bush. Credit a different phrasing if the explanation is supported by the passage.

**Useful evidence:** “apologised to the deer for his guess”; “a strong gust blew through the trees”; or “A clue is not the same as proof.”

### Task 3 — Event sequence

1. Piku notices hoofprints and makes a guess.
2. A strip of red cloth is traced past the thorn and fallen log.
3. The marker is found wrapped around a bush.
4. Piku apologises and reties the marker.

Accept equivalent descriptions that preserve the supported chronological order. Do not require word-for-word reproduction.

### Task 4 — Location prepositions

1. beside
2. under
3. across
4. around
5. behind

Each answer should match the spatial relationship in the sentence. If a learner proposes an alternative preposition, judge whether it is grammatical and consistent with the passage rather than marking solely by memorised list.

## 3. Internal formative guide — 12 points (not an official CBSE mark scheme)

| Part | Points | Evidence of success |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Direct clue | 1 | Selects the red-cloth trail as the relevant locating evidence |
| 2. Inference | 2 | Gives a plausible explanation (1); supports it with an accurate passage detail (1) |
| 3. Sequence | 2 | All four events in supported order (2); one adjacent reversal or partial order with at least two events correctly placed (1) |
| 4. Prepositions | 4 | One point each for four of the five correctly contextualised choices; cap at 4 |
| 5. Retelling | 3 | Coherent order (1); relevant evidence/lesson about unsupported guessing (1); at least two correct location prepositions (1) |

The cap in Part 4 is deliberate: the fifth item is extra evidence/checking, not an additional point. Total formative maximum is 12. This is a platform-created diagnostic aid; it is not a CBSE marks scheme, grade conversion or prediction.

## 4. Feedback and misconceptions

- **Hoofprints prove guilt:** Explain that a clue may point to a possibility without proving who caused an event.
- **Retelling copies the story without explaining the change:** Ask the learner to connect Piku's apology to the new information.
- **Events listed in random order:** Have the learner identify what was discovered before the marker was found.
- **Prepositions treated as interchangeable:** Ask the learner to visualise the object-location relationship and reread the sentence.
- **Overly certain inference:** Encourage wording such as “the detail suggests…” and ask which passage detail supports the view.
- **Different but supported interpretation:** Accept it. Do not force one moral response when the learner's reasoning matches the text.

## 5. Support and extension

- **Support:** Provide the event names on separate cards to order; allow oral rehearsal; reread the relevant sentence; give a small word bank for location terms.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to explain why hoofprints were insufficient evidence and distinguish the first guess from what the search later established.
- **Accessibility:** Instructions should remain visible beside the response field. Use plain text and semantic labels after integration. Do not rely on red/green colour coding; the red marker is identified by words. Accept typing or speech-to-text if the eventual interface supports it.
