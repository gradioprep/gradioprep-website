## 1. Intended learning evidence

Learners should locate a relevant clue, explain an inference from the story, reconstruct the event sequence, use common location prepositions in context, and retell a short narrative coherently. These are internal learning goals informed by public source context, not official CBSE outcome wording.

## 2. Suggested answers and rationale

### Task 1 — Direct clue

**Expected choice:** B — the loose wooden wedge is behind a broad root. The scrape in the soil leads down the slope to that displaced piece.

**Rationale:** Hoofprints establish that a deer passed near the post; they do not establish that the deer moved the arrow. The displaced wedge and scrape provide the explanation in the story.

### Task 2 — Inference

**Strong response characteristics:** Lali recognises that she guessed the deer was responsible before checking the ground. The fallen branch knocked the wedge loose, and the wind turned the arrow. Credit other wording if the learner uses a relevant passage detail and makes the reasoning explicit.

**Useful evidence:** The line that a fallen branch knocked the wedge from its slot; the detail that hoofprints were near the post; or Lali’s apology to the deer.

### Task 3 — Event sequence

1. Lali notices hoofprints and makes a guess.
2. The scrape in the soil leads down the slope.
3. The loose wooden wedge is found behind a broad root.
4. Lali apologises and resets the arrow.

Accept equivalent descriptions that preserve the supported chronological order. Do not require word-for-word reproduction.

### Task 4 — Location prepositions

1. at
2. near
3. down
4. behind
5. beside

Each answer should match the spatial relationship in the sentence. If a learner proposes a different preposition, check whether it is grammatical and consistent with the described location.

## 3. Internal formative guide — 12 points (not an official CBSE mark scheme)

| Part | Points | Evidence of success |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Direct clue | 1 | Selects the displaced wedge as the evidence that explains the turn |
| 2. Inference | 2 | Gives a plausible explanation (1); supports it with an accurate passage detail (1) |
| 3. Sequence | 2 | All four events in supported order (2); a mostly correct order with one reversal (1) |
| 4. Prepositions | 4 | Correctly contextualises four or more of the five location words; cap at 4 |
| 5. Retelling | 3 | Coherent order (1); uses evidence to explain Lali’s change (1); uses at least two correct location prepositions (1) |

The cap in Part 4 is deliberate: the fifth item is an additional check, not an extra point. Total formative maximum is 12. This is a platform-created classroom aid, not a CBSE marks scheme, grade conversion or prediction.

## 4. Feedback and misconceptions

- **Hoofprints prove that the deer moved the arrow:** Explain that tracks show presence, not necessarily the cause of the arrow turning.
- **Retelling the plot without explaining Lali’s change:** Ask the learner to connect the displaced wedge and gust of wind with the apology.
- **Events listed in random order:** Ask which observation led to the wedge being found.
- **Prepositions treated as interchangeable:** Ask the learner to visualise the object-location relationship and reread the sentence.
- **Overly certain inference:** Encourage wording such as “the detail suggests…” and ask which story detail supports the view.
- **Different but supported interpretation:** Accept it when it fits the evidence; do not force a single moral statement.

## 5. Support and extension

- **Support:** Provide the event names on separate cards to order; allow oral rehearsal; reread the relevant sentence; provide a location-word bank.
- **Extension:** Ask learners to explain why hoofprints were insufficient evidence and distinguish Lali’s first guess from the explanation the search established.
- **Accessibility:** Keep instructions visible beside response fields after integration. Use plain text and semantic labels. Do not rely on colour coding; the arrow is described in words. Accept typing or speech-to-text if supported by the eventual interface.
