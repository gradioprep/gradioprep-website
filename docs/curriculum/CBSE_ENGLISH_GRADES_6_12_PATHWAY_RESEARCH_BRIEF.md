## 1. Product decision

Add CBSE English Grades 6–12 as a new pathway family alongside the existing IB and Cambridge English pathways. This is an addition of curriculum scope only. It does **not** change the product architecture, page hierarchy, unit structure, research method, curriculum-rotation pattern, implementation workflow, approval requirements or content-release process.

The binding product architecture remains:

**Pathways catalogue → curriculum/course → class/level → unit → lesson/practice.**

Use the same reusable platform templates and instructional engine as the other tracks, while keeping CBSE's own learning outcomes, texts, skills progression, sequence, assessment formats and official mapping separate. Never copy an IB or Cambridge unit plan and relabel it CBSE.

## 2. Classes and course distinctions to research

- **Grades 6–8:** Middle-stage English. Establish the current NCERT textbook and syllabus position for each grade, relevant NCF-SE 2023 learning standards/competencies, CBSE guidance and any current transition or bridge materials. Do not assume a single independent CBSE class-wise syllabus PDF exists for each of these grades.
- **Grades 9–10:** Secondary-stage English. Use the applicable academic-session CBSE curriculum, current NCERT syllabus/textbooks and any relevant changes arising from NCF-SE implementation. Preserve course-choice distinctions such as English Language and Literature and English Communicative wherever each is officially offered/applicable. Do not assume a route's code or availability for both classes without checking current official sources.
- **Grades 11–12:** Senior-secondary English. Research English Core and English Elective separately wherever applicable. Keep subject code, prescribed texts, paper structure, assessment components and academic-session syllabus edition distinct.

The pathway label “CBSE English Grades 6–12” is a product-family label, not a claim that CBSE prescribes one identical English course across all seven classes. Exact course destinations must follow the current official class/session evidence.

## 3. Current-source baseline and currency requirement

Research checked on 26 October 2026 identified these official starting points:

1. [CBSE Curriculum 2026–27](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/curriculum_2027.html) — official portal for Classes IX–X and XI–XII curriculum, including English options listed for those stages.
2. [CBSE latest circulars](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/circulars.html) — includes 2026–27 curriculum and language-scheme notices, including circulars on the secondary curriculum's alignment with NCERT syllabus and the introduction of R3 in Class VI. The R3 change concerns the third-language scheme; it must not be misrepresented as a change to English.
3. [NCF-SE 2023](https://ncert.gov.in/pdf/focus-group/NCF-SE_2023EN.pdf) and [NCF framework portal](https://ncf.ncert.gov.in/) — framework context for learning standards, competency development, pedagogy and assessment.
4. [NCERT textbooks portal](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php) — check the actual textbook and edition for each grade; record title, grade, publication/reprint details and availability.
5. [NCERT/Ministry of Education statement on syllabus alignment, 10 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297094&lang=2&reg=48) — states that NCERT has brought out new syllabus and textbooks for Grades 1–9 and that these are developed through mapping of curricular goals, competencies and learning outcomes with academic, pedagogical, language and sensitivity review.

These are starting points, not a completed syllabus audit. Before developing any class guide, verify the latest accessible official documents for that exact grade and academic session. Record effective date, revision/version, applicable textbook and assessment information. Do not infer that every grade changed in the same way or that a recent national framework automatically overrides a current class-specific CBSE syllabus without an official basis.

## 4. Required research and design method

Follow the binding IB/Cambridge curriculum research workflow, extended to CBSE by the update recorded in the workflow. For each grade and applicable course route:

1. Identify the current official CBSE/NCERT curriculum, textbook, learning outcomes/competencies, syllabus edition and assessment rules.
2. Trace relevant NCF-SE 2023 principles to grade-specific official sources rather than applying broad principles as if they were exact syllabus requirements.
3. Compare relevant India-focused and international English EdTech/teaching platforms and publishers at the same age/grade band. Review only what can be verified publicly: progression, unit architecture, text types, modelling, practice, feedback, assessments, differentiation, accessibility and teacher/learner workflow.
4. Cross-check proposed pedagogy against reputable evidence and the public-source register. Use platform research to inform implementation quality, not to determine official CBSE requirements.
5. Design a distinct grade-specific curriculum guide and unit sequence, with source traceability and explicit confidence/status for each mapping.
6. Keep official codes/wording only where directly verified. Otherwise use clearly labelled internal skill outcomes and mark the official mapping unresolved.
7. Keep academic/content review, official mapping/currency, rights/originality, accessibility, age/cultural suitability and release approval separate.

## 5. Binding Grade 6-equivalent Unit 1 order

The first cross-track cohort is fixed as follows:

1. **IB MYP Language and Literature Year 1 — Unit 1.**
2. **Cambridge Lower Secondary English 0861 Stage 7 — Unit 1.** Stage 7 is typically for ages 11–12; grade alignment varies by school/country, so this is a planning comparison rather than a universal equivalence.
3. **CBSE English Grade 6 — Unit 1.**

Finish the student-facing Unit 1 content and record the required academic/content, rights/originality, accessibility, age/cultural suitability and release checks for each route before moving to older-level Unit 1 work or any Unit 2. Official mapping/currency is a separate status; never invent it. If one of the three is blocked, pause and report the blocker instead of skipping ahead. This rule supersedes earlier examples or lists that put DP, IGCSE or higher grades ahead of CBSE Grade 6.

After the first cohort, the next production cohort is Unit 1 for IB MYP Year 2 → Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 8 → CBSE Grade 7, subject to verifying actual age/grade alignment and current sources. Continue with Unit 1 across subsequent comparable bands (for example MYP Year 3 → Cambridge Stage 9 → CBSE Grade 8). Do not start Unit 2 for the first three pathways immediately after their Unit 1s finish; Unit 2 begins only after Unit 1 is complete across all approved in-scope levels. The platform's architecture, unit structure, research method and release process do not change. Keep the exact programme/course/stage/grade identity separate for every unit; the cohort is a production order, not a claim that the curricula share objectives or content.

## 8. Grade 6 Unit 1 candidate source check — 28 October 2026

### Direct official public references checked

1. [NCERT advisory for schools](https://www.ncert.nic.in/pdf/announcement/NCERT-Advisory-for-Schools.pdf) lists **Poorvi** as the Grade 6 English textbook. This confirms textbook identity at the level evidenced by the advisory; it does not pin the exact current reprint/edition for every school.
2. [NCERT CIET Grade 6 activity list](https://www.ciet.ncert.gov.in/activity/grade6?lang=en) lists *Poorvi*, Chapter 1 **“Fables and Folk Tales”**, with activities **“Learning Prepositions of Location”** and **“Learning Prepositions of Location—Picture Dictation”**. This provides a useful thematic/design context for the candidate, not authority to copy the textbook passage or to assign an unsupported official objective code.
3. [NCERT online textbook portal and copyright terms](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php) state that NCERT textbooks are copyrighted and that republication, redistribution or incorporation into digital content packages/software is prohibited under the published terms absent the stated permission. Therefore, this candidate uses a short, platform-original passage and newly written tasks. Do not embed or reproduce textbook prose, exercise wording, illustrations, scans or screenshots.
4. The [Ministry of Education / PIB public statement dated 10 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2297094&lang=2&reg=48) describes NCF-SE 2023 as a basis for stage/subject curricular goals, competencies, outcomes, syllabi, textbooks, pedagogy and assessment, and notes newly issued NCERT textbooks through Grade 9. Treat this as context requiring current grade-level checks, not as a substitute for an exact English descriptor.

### Candidate design boundary

The current draft, **“The Missing Bell,”** uses a short original animal narrative to teach reading a story for events, clues and supported inference, plus a brief activity on prepositions of location. It does not reproduce the named *Poorvi* stories or their exercise wording. No external image/photo, third-party excerpt, textbook scan or copied NCERT asset is included.

**Mapping state:** unverified. No official CBSE objective/competency code is assigned. Before any mapping claim or release, confirm the applicable current NCERT textbook reprint and the appropriate Grade 6 English learning outcome/competency in an exact official source. Do not infer a code from the activity page alone.

**Source/rights state:** the lesson's short text and tasks are platform-original and have received a limited public distinctive-phrase screen with no exact passage-level match found in returned results. The unit is not yet human-approved. The exact-source and no-copy rule remains binding for future sourced text/media. The separate QA record logs the review scope and limitations.
