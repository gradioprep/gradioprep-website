## 3. Internal originality/provenance desk screen — 28 October 2026

The initial version, titled “The Red Marker,” used a red-cloth clue beside a thorn and the general sentence “A clue is not the same as proof.” A supplementary search surfaced an unrelated public article with very similar generic headline wording and a separate fiction result describing a red strand tangled on a thorn. Neither was an exact copy of the initial story, but these potential wording/imagery overlaps were unnecessary. Before review, the story was changed to “The Turning Sign,” in which a wooden direction arrow turns after a wedge is knocked loose.

For the revised passage, three distinctive phrase searches were run:
- “Lali the langur noticed hoofprints near the post”
- “A fallen branch had knocked the wedge from its slot”
- “Hoofprints show that a deer passed here”

No exact match to the revised passage surfaced in the returned public search results. One result discussed hoofprints as natural signs; it did not reproduce this passage. No third-party source was intentionally used to write the revised story.

**Result:** a limited internal originality/provenance screen is recorded as **PASS for the revised draft text**, scoped to these phrases and the returned public results only. It is not comprehensive plagiarism detection, proof of authorship/exclusivity, or a legal opinion. Recheck if the text changes materially or a concrete concern appears.

## 5. Content/accessibility desk checks

- The passage is short, divided into manageable paragraphs and uses a clear event sequence.
- It includes location words in meaningful contexts, rather than an isolated word list only.
- The main inference task explicitly distinguishes a clue from the conclusion about who moved the sign.
- The draft uses selectable text and does not rely on colour to convey the correct response.
- The practice progression moves from direct information to inference, sequence, language in context and independent retelling.
