**Review version:** 1.0

**Route:** Cambridge Lower Secondary English (0861), Stage 7  
**Learner band:** Stage 7 is typically for learners aged 11–12; school-grade correspondence varies.  
**Purpose:** Complete the route-specific Top Five independent-source audit and record design implications for the Stage 7 guide and Unit 1.  
**Status:** **Top Five complete.** This is a research and design review only. It does not revise the v1.3 learner or teacher content, add counters, approve a new content version, verify the complete current framework, clear rights or release the unit.

## 1. Executive decision

Five independent providers have been ranked using the binding Section 2A rubric in `docs/IB_CAMBRIDGE_CURRICULUM_RESEARCH_WORKFLOW.md`. Two are directly relevant resources for Cambridge Lower Secondary English 0861 and are currently listed by Cambridge International: Collins Cambridge Lower Secondary English (Second edition) and Hodder Education's Cambridge Checkpoint Lower Secondary English (Third edition). Three are age-band comparators with useful, inspectable English-teaching approaches for learners around 11–14: Pearson Inspire English International, Oxford International Lower Secondary English, and the English & Media Centre (EMC) 11–14 resource library.

**Important distinction:** only the first two are 0861-specific resources in Cambridge's published resource list. The other three are not Cambridge-endorsed and do not establish Cambridge syllabus requirements, objective codes or Stage 7 coverage. They are included because the workflow asks for the strongest independent implementation comparisons and they supply age-appropriate teaching, modelling, text, differentiation or teacher-workflow evidence. A shared MYP resource list has not been reused.

The comparison supports the current Stage 7 guide's broad design: explicit modelling, a progression from guided to independent reading work, a varied text diet across the full stage, differentiated access, and direct evidence of speaking/listening elsewhere in the stage sequence. It does **not** justify turning Unit 1 into a full four-skill assessment or adding Cambridge codes beyond the narrow public 7Ri.03 cross-check already recorded for explicit-information items.

## 3. Scoring method

Each candidate is scored from 0 to 5 on five dimensions. Weighted total is the sum of each dimension score multiplied by its weight. Scores are internal comparative judgements, not accreditation, an external ranking or evidence of official Cambridge approval.

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Exact curriculum, subject and learner-level relevance | 30% |
| Named expertise, authorship and source transparency | 20% |
| Currency and visible update/version evidence | 15% |
| Quality of publicly inspectable evidence | 20% |
| Usefulness for teaching, assessment or implementation design | 15% |

All five pages/samples below were checked on **31 October 2026**. Publicly inspectable evidence is distinguished from paid, subscription-only or otherwise uninspected materials.

### 1. Collins — Cambridge Lower Secondary English (0861), Second edition, Stage 7

**Direct fit:** Exact 0861 Stage 7 student resource and currently listed on Cambridge's public English 0861 resource page.  
**Pages inspected:** [Collins Stage 7 Student's Book product page](https://collins.co.uk/products/9780008340834) and Cambridge's [official 0861 resource listing](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-lower-secondary/resource-list/cambridge-lower-secondary-english-0861/). The product page identifies the Second edition, ISBN 978-0-00-834083-4 and publication date 14 January 2021.  
**Authorship/transparency:** Series edited by Julia Burchell and Mike Gould; named contributing authors include Lucy Birchenough, Clare Constant, Steve Eddy, Naomi Hursthouse, Ian Kirby, Nikki Smith and Richard Vardy. The page gives brief professional biographies for the series editors.  
**Inspectable contribution:** The page describes a text-purpose sequence: learners explore texts, complete scaffolded writing and speaking/listening activities, and work towards meaningful final tasks. It identifies Stage 7 purposes including describing, informing, persuading/advising, narrating, reviewing/discussing, and exploring/commenting. It also reports models/examples, end-of-chapter self-assessment, varied international fiction/poetry/drama/non-fiction, increasing text challenge, and lesson-by-lesson teacher usability. The Cambridge listing independently confirms the current resource-list placement and describes the series as skills-focused with differentiation.  
**Limits/access:** The public product description and official resource listing were inspected; the full textbook, Teacher's Guide and paid digital resources were not inspected. The product page's claim of framework coverage is a publisher description, while Cambridge's inclusion on the official resource list is the awarding-body cross-check. Neither gives permission to reproduce book content.  
**Scores:** 5.0 relevance; 4.8 expertise; 4.7 currency; 4.6 inspectable evidence; 4.9 usefulness. **Weighted 4.82/5.**  
**Design decision:** Strongest exact-route reference for a coherent Stage 7 progression across text purposes and the four skills. Use its progression and modelling pattern as a design comparator only; do not copy its chapter order, extracts, prompts, examples or assessment items.

### 2. Hachette Learning / Hodder Education — Cambridge Checkpoint Lower Secondary English Student's Book 7, Third edition

**Direct fit:** Exact 0861 Stage 7 title, Third edition, currently listed on Cambridge's public resource list.  
**Pages/samples inspected:** [Hachette product page](https://www.hachettelearning.com/english/cambridge-checkpoint-lower-secondary-english-student-s-book-7); [public sample PDF](https://media.hachettelearning.com/media/medialibraries/hodder/samples/english/11-14/cambridge-checkpoint-lower-secondary-english/cambridge-checkpoint-lower-secondary-english-student-s-book-7-sample-pages-9781398300163.pdf); Cambridge's [official 0861 resource list](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-lower-secondary/resource-list/cambridge-lower-secondary-english-0861/). The product page records the paperback publication date as 25 June 2021 (ISBN 9781398300163) and the Third edition Boost eBook date as 27 July 2021.  
**Authorship/transparency:** The sample identifies John Reynolds as author. The current product listing identifies the Stage 7 title, format, edition and publication details.  
**Inspectable contribution:** The sample contents cover fact, growing up, painting with words, news, stories, short stories, global cultures, poetry and drama. Its sample pages show varied reading and speaking/listening prompts, cultural-context discussion, writing tasks (including letters and haiku), word-attack skills, direct speech, parts of speech and prepositions. This makes it useful for text-type breadth, cultural comparison and linking language knowledge to reading/writing.  
**Currency and sample caveat:** The public sample PDF is an older pre-publication sample and includes an earlier statement that endorsement was still being sought. That wording is not used as the current endorsement decision. Cambridge's current public resource list now lists the Third edition under English 0861. The product page shows the 2021 publication dates.  
**Limits/access:** The student-book sample is public but is not a full chapter. The Teacher's Guide and Boost subscription material are not publicly inspected; their contents are not inferred. The sample contains publisher-protected material and is not a source for copying.  
**Scores:** 5.0 relevance; 4.7 expertise; 4.3 currency; 4.7 inspectable evidence; 4.7 usefulness. **Weighted 4.73/5.**  
**Design decision:** Useful comparator for international voices and text-form diversity, vocabulary access and connecting reading to speaking/listening and writing. Preserve age-appropriate cultural context and avoid treating any single story as representative of a country or community. Do not convert its activities or text into platform questions.

### 3. Pearson — Inspire English International, Year 7

### 4. Oxford University Press — Oxford International Lower Secondary English, Year 7

**Fit:** International Year 7 / lower-secondary comparator. The public product page identifies student books and teacher materials for Years 7–9, but the accessible page does not establish direct mapping to Cambridge 0861.  
**Pages inspected:** [Oxford International Lower Secondary English product page](https://www.oup.es/oxford-international-lower-secondary-english/). It names six authors: Alison Barber, Rachel Redford, Eve Sullivan, Emma Danihel, Mark Saunders and Patricia Mertin.  
**Inspectable contribution:** OUP describes an enquiry-based approach, global themes, collaboration, and comprehension questions differentiated into retrieval (A), language techniques (B) and inference (C). It also mentions grammar tips, word-origin vocabulary boxes and Teacher's Guides with lesson guidance. The page lists Year 7 Student Book, Workbook, Teacher's Guide and separate Kerboodle subscriptions. A sample lesson link was visible for Year 8, not a publicly inspected Year 7 chapter.  
**Limits/access:** Year 7 product architecture and the publisher's public methodology description were inspected; the Year 7 book and Teacher's Guide are not freely inspectable through the page, and Kerboodle is separately purchased. No official Cambridge alignment is claimed.  
**Scores:** 3.5 relevance; 4.8 expertise; 4.2 currency; 4.3 inspectable evidence; 4.4 usefulness. **Weighted 4.16/5.**  
**Design decision:** A useful example of differentiated question demand, enquiry and global themes, and a way to offer retrieval/language/inference support without labelling a learner by one score. Use the underlying differentiation principle only; do not adopt its A/B/C labels as Cambridge codes or claim its sequence as official.

## 5. Cross-check against pedagogy evidence

The Education Endowment Foundation's secondary literacy guidance covers reading, writing, talk, vocabulary and supporting struggling students; its reading-comprehension strategy summary describes conscious modelling of strategies followed by guided, collaborative or individual application and gradual release to independence. See [Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-ks3-ks4) (published 6 July 2018) and [Reading comprehension strategies](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/reading-comprehension-strategies) (public Toolkit page checked 31 October 2026). These are pedagogy cross-checks, not Cambridge requirements or source-ranking entries.

The research supports the current Stage 7 approach of making reasoning visible, using precise clues, explaining why evidence supports an inference, and fading support from worked example to guided and independent work. It also supports careful vocabulary/text selection and feedback that diagnoses whether the learner's difficulty is literal understanding, inference, evidence selection or explanation.

## 6. Design implications for the Stage 7 guide and Unit 1

| Design area | Adopt / retain | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Whole-stage skills | Keep reading, writing, speaking and listening integrated across the Stage 7 guide. Unit 1 may remain reading-focused; later units should gather direct writing and oral/listening evidence. | Do not claim Unit 1 alone covers all four skills or that a book's sequence is Cambridge's required order. |
| Reading sequence | Keep explicit meaning → supported inference → precise evidence → explanation of language/detail. Model the reasoning, then use guided and independent practice. | Use original tasks/text; do not copy published examples or question wording. |
| Text range | Include fiction, poetry, playscripts and non-fiction across the full stage plan, with international and varied voices. | Unit 1 does not need every text type; do not stereotype cultures or assume one text represents a country. |
| Differentiation | Use progressive question demands, vocabulary support, scaffolded frames and multiple ways to record observations. | Do not import OUP A/B/C labels or any publisher's scoring scheme as Cambridge objectives. |
| Teacher pack | For each objective item, explain the answer in its exact context and why each alternative fails; for ordering, explain sequence logic. For typed responses include task-specific range, ideal target, model, evidence criteria, acceptable interpretations and actionable feedback. | This is GradiPrep's adopted standard, not a claim that publishers use the same template. The current Stage 7 v1.3 pack still needs the scheduled migration. |
| Assessment | Keep Stage 7 practice low-stakes, diagnostic and formative; measure speaking/listening directly when the task requires it. | Do not style Unit 1 as an official Progression Test or Checkpoint mock. |
| Rights | Use published sources only to study publicly described structure and pedagogy. | Do not reproduce textbook text, workbook questions, model answers, page layouts, teacher-guide materials or restricted test content. Existing short original passages remain within the 250-word rule. |

Weighted scores are reasoned internal judgements based on what was visible, not objective quality ratings.

Do not fill these gaps using publisher descriptions, third-party mirrors or inference.
